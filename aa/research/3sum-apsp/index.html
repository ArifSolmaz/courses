    Where does this fit in the course?

    These are our course week numbers, not section numbers in the research paper. Some ideas are foundations you have already studied; others are previews of later weeks. You can read the small examples now and revisit the deeper connections later.

    Week 1 · Correctness

    Foundation. A successful example is not a proof. The new method must give correct answers for every input allowed by its assumptions.

    Case-study pieces 1–2 and 16

    Weeks 2–3 · Growth and analysis

    Central connection. Understand n, Big-O, constants and exponents. Distinguish a known upper bound from a claim that no faster method exists.

    Pieces 3–6 and 13 · Week 3 notes

    Week 7 · Recursion and counting work

    Central technique. Follow a recursive calculation, keep the branches needed for requested outputs, and count all remaining work. The paper’s algebra is more advanced than our sorting examples.

    Pieces 10–13

    Week 8 · Graph representations

    Foundation. Vertices, edges and adjacency matrices let us express shared neighbours and triangles as calculations on tables.

    Pieces 8–10

    Week 11 · Shortest paths

    Direct problem connection. APSP asks for shortest distances between every pair of vertices. The factory-route example explains the question the new algorithm solves.

    Pieces 4, 6 and 8

    Week 14 · Reductions and complexity

    Central significance. Reductions carry a faster solver from one problem to another. Here the question is a better polynomial exponent, not whether an NP-complete problem suddenly becomes easy.

    Pieces 5, 7–8, 15 and 17

    Week 5 · Data structures

    Supporting connection and entry point. Fast hash lookups can still be repeated quadratically many times. The paper also has a preprocessing/query result. It does not claim a breakthrough in ordinary BST or hash-table operations.

    Pieces 3 and 14

    Weeks 12–13 · Reusing work

    Helpful analogy. Dynamic programming helps us recognize repeated work and dependencies. The main thin-matrix technique is not simply our familiar memoization algorithm.

    Pieces 11–14 · Week 13 applications

    New material beyond the course: fast algebraic matrix multiplication, thin-matrix parameter choices and the detailed counting proof. The case study introduces their purpose; it does not replace the full proof.

    Paper map: pieces 1–6: problems and claims; 7–8: reductions; 9–13: matrix idea and proof outline; 14: queries and data structures; 15–18: significance, evidence and engineering lessons.
