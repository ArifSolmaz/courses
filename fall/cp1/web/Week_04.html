The event between the samples

01 / The situation

Does a log containing no alarm prove there was no event?

A simulated signal is high for 200 ≤ t < 300 ms and low at all other times. You choose a finite sampling schedule between 0 and 500 ms. Your task is to report exactly what the samples show—and avoid claiming more.

Your handover

A time-axis sketch, two loop traces and a carefully limited conclusion about event detection.

Start on paper. Work alone or in a pair: one person predicts, the other challenges the assumptions; swap roles halfway through. All essential data is provided. No hardware or AI account is required.

02 / Build a model

Understand what the code represents.

A loop defines the observations

range(start, stop, step) includes start and excludes stop. range(0, 500, 100) visits 0, 100, 200, 300 and 400. A missing endpoint can change both the sample count and the interpretation of a test.

Timing changes the evidence

Sampling at 0, 250 and 500 ms detects this pulse. Sampling at 0, 300 and 600 ms misses it. Those observations describe two schedules, not two different physical events.

Simulation time is not wall-clock time

Incrementing t_ms in a notebook does not schedule a real sensor at that interval. Here, a loop explores a mathematical timeline. Real acquisition would also involve timing accuracy, latency and sensor behaviour.

Inspect the small Python model

Read and predict first. Run this self-contained example in a new notebook cell; use the result as evidence to discuss.

hits = 0
for t_ms in range(0, 501, 100):
    high = 200 <= t_ms < 300
    print(t_ms, high)
    if high:
        hits = hits + 1
print('Detected samples:', hits)

03 / Predict & inspect

Try to break your first explanation.

These are prepared teaching cases, not live sensor readings or an automatic grade. Write a prediction before opening a case, then reveal the reasoning one step at a time. Explain any disagreement.

01100 ms schedule

t = 0, 100, 200, 300, 400, 500 ms

Predict: How many high samples? Is 300 ms inside the pulse?

  1. 0, 100 → low
  2. 200 → high
  3. 300, 400, 500 → low

1 high sample out of 6

The pulse excludes 300 ms. Six observations do not reveal the entire continuous signal.

02300 ms schedule

t = 0, 300 ms within the 0–500 ms observation window

Predict: Will this schedule detect the pulse?

  1. 0 < 200 → low
  2. 300 is outside [200, 300) → low
  3. No sample lies inside the pulse

0 high samples out of 2

No detection is not proof of no event. The pulse existed between observations.

03Shifted schedule

t = 50, 250, 450 ms

Predict: Can fewer samples still detect this particular pulse?

  1. 50 → low
  2. 250 → high
  3. 450 → low

1 high sample out of 3

Phase matters as well as interval. This example does not establish a universal sampling guarantee.

Change the observations

Sampling bench

The pulse is high for 200 ≤ t < 300 ms. Samples start at the chosen offset and continue through 500 ms if the schedule lands there. This is simulated time.

Change interval first, then offset. A detected sample is marked HIGH; the log cannot establish what happened at unsampled times.

04 / Investigate

One question. Several kinds of evidence.

  1. Draw the pulse on a time axis. Mark each sampling time before executing the loop.
  2. Trace the count by hand for 100 ms and 300 ms intervals. Explain why range ends at 501 in the supplied model.
  3. Move the pulse to 210 ≤ t < 290 ms. Predict which of the same schedules now misses it, then adapt the code.
  4. Write two report sentences: one supported by the samples and one tempting claim that the samples cannot support.

AI as a collaborator / You own the judgment

Review the answer, not its confidence.

Ask AI whether zero high samples proves the signal stayed low. Supply the pulse and 300 ms schedule as a counterexample. Check its proposed loop endpoints and its distinction between simulated and real time.

Without AI, review the supplied model or exchange an implementation with a partner. The same standard applies: state the claim, find a counterexample, and explain the correction. AI practice here does not change exam or demonstration rules.

06 / Defend the result

If you can afford only three observations, what additional information would help you place them?

Give a brief technical explanation using your own results. A useful handover contains a claim with units, one independent check, one boundary or fault case, and one limitation. More lines of code or more completed cells are not substitutes for this evidence.
