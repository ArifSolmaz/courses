Why the ISS stays up. At v = √(GM/r) the projectile falls toward Earth exactly as fast as the surface curves away beneath it, so it falls forever without getting closer. That is all an orbit is.

Plane wave E = E₀ x̂ cos(kz − ωt), B = (E₀/v) ŷ cos(kz − ωt) with v = c/n; in vacuum this becomes B = E₀/c. The B amplitude is drawn ×c so it is visible. Green arrows: the Poynting vector S = E×B/μ₀, always along +z and proportional to cos².

Light is a self-propagating field

A changing E makes a curling B, a changing B makes a curling E, and in vacuum the pair moves at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀). Send the wave into glass and watch v = c/n while the frequency stays fixed.

Frequency f = c/λ₀
Speed in medium v = c/n
Wavelength in medium λ₀/n
Wave impedance η = E/H
Entering a medium changes the wavelength and the speed but never the frequency: the source, not the medium, sets f.
Where this matters. The same three vectors describe a Wi-Fi signal at 12 cm, a fibre-optic pulse at 1550 nm, and a radar return. Antenna length, coax impedance (η₀ ≈ 377 Ω) and fibre bandwidth all follow from them.

Working fluid: 1 mol of an ideal monatomic gas (γ = 5/3). Isotherms PV = nRT and adiabats PV^γ = const are computed, not sketched. On the T–S diagram the cycle is a rectangle, and η is the ratio of the rectangle's area to the area under its top edge.

Why atoms have colours

An electron can only sit on allowed rungs. Jump down and a photon carrying exactly the energy difference leaves; jump up and a photon of exactly that energy must arrive.
