Course Description

This calculus-based physics course teaches the prediction of mechanical systems through Python-enhanced simulations. Given any mechanical system (falling, sliding, rotating, oscillating), students learn to draw the diagram, identify the right principle (Newton's laws, energy conservation, momentum conservation), write the equation, predict the outcome, and verify with simulation.

The course follows a consistent methodology: Diagram → Principle → Equation → Prediction → Verify. Each week includes a Lecture notebook and a Problem Lab notebook delivered via Google Colab.

Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

  1. Perform dimensional analysis and vector decomposition as the foundation for all physics predictions.
  2. Describe and predict 1D and 2D motion using kinematic equations and graphical analysis.
  3. Apply Newton's laws and free body diagrams to predict the motion of systems with multiple forces.
  4. Use energy and momentum conservation as shortcut principles for predicting outcomes of complex interactions.
  5. Analyze rotational systems using torque, moment of inertia, and angular momentum conservation.
  6. Model simple harmonic motion and predict resonance conditions in oscillating systems.
  7. Describe wave propagation, interference, and standing wave patterns using superposition principles.
  8. Complete a capstone project that models a mechanical system, predicts its behavior, and verifies with simulation.

Notebook Grading Criteria

  • Completeness: All exercise cells attempted and submitted via the auto-submission system
  • Correctness: Predictions match expected outcomes; simulations verify analytical solutions
  • Methodology: Proper use of Diagram → Principle → Equation → Prediction → Verify workflow
  • Problem-solving approach: Evidence of systematic physical reasoning, not just formula plugging

Course Policies

Attendance

Regular attendance is expected. Students who miss more than 30% of classes may be denied the right to take the final exam, per university regulations. Notebook submissions serve as an additional record of engagement.

Late Submission

Weekly notebooks must be submitted by the deadline shown in each notebook. Late submissions may receive a penalty of 10% per day, up to 3 days. After 3 days, late submissions are not accepted unless a valid excuse is provided.

Academic Honesty

All submitted work must be your own. You may discuss general approaches with classmates, but the solutions you submit must be written independently. The following are considered violations:

  • Copying solutions from another student or external source without attribution
  • Sharing your exercise solutions with other students
  • Submitting work generated entirely by AI tools without understanding and modification

AI Tool Usage

AI assistants (ChatGPT, Copilot, etc.) may be used as learning aids to understand concepts. However, you must be able to explain and modify any solution you submit. Exercises are designed to build your physics intuition progressively — bypassing them with AI defeats the purpose and will leave you unprepared for exams.

Communication
