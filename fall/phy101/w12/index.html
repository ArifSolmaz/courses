Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  1. Calculate the center of mass for systems of point masses and extended objects
  2. Apply the center of mass concept to analyze motion of multi-body systems
  3. Define torque and compute it for various force-lever arm configurations
  4. State the two conditions for static equilibrium ($\Sigma F = 0$, $\Sigma \tau = 0$)
  5. Solve static equilibrium problems for beams, levers, and structures
  6. Apply equilibrium analysis to engineering problems (robot arms, bridges, cranes)

Joining this lessonDerse buradan başlayanlar

You can join with a force diagram and a calculator; no earlier Python work is needed. Recall these three ideas:

  • Weight is $W=mg$, downward. For a uniform beam it acts at the midpoint; $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ here.
  • In a planar static problem, $\sum F_x=0$, $\sum F_y=0$ and $\sum\tau=0$. Choose a pivot and one positive turning direction.
  • Torque is $\tau=F d_\perp$, using the perpendicular distance to the force's line. For a downward weight on an inclined rod, $d_\perp=r\cos\theta$ when $\theta$ is measured above horizontal.

Quick entry check: A $100\,\mathrm N$ load acts $0.40\,\mathrm m$ right of a pivot. An upward force at $0.80\,\mathrm m$ on the same side balances it when $F(0.80)=100(0.40)$, giving $F=50\,\mathrm N$. Then use force balance to find the pivot reaction.

Before the equations: a force can turn an objectKuvvet ve döndürme etkisi

Work on paper first; the interactive pictures are optional ways to test a prediction. For a body at rest we need both $\sum F_x=0,\ \sum F_y=0$ and $\sum\tau=0$. A zero net force alone does not prevent rotation.

Draw every force at its application point. Replace a uniform rod's distributed weight by $mg$ at its midpoint. Choose a pivot that removes an unknown force: a force whose line passes through the pivot has zero lever arm.

The geometry bridge: $\tau=F d_\perp=rF\sin\theta$. The lever arm $d_\perp$ is the shortest distance from the pivot to the line of the force, not always the length of the object. A vertical weight on a rod at angle $\phi$ above horizontal has lever arm $r\cos\phi$. A horizontal force at its end has lever arm $r\sin\phi$.

The algebra bridge: If $F(4\,\mathrm m)=(200\,\mathrm N)(2\,\mathrm m)$, divide both sides by $4\,\mathrm m$ to get $F=100\,\mathrm N$. The metres cancel. A negative reaction force means the real direction is opposite to the arrow you guessed; a pin can push or pull, but a simple support cannot pull downward.

For center of mass, first calculate each $m_ix_i$ and $m_iy_i$, add each column, then divide by total mass: $x_{\rm cm}=\frac{\sum_i m_ix_i}{\sum_i m_i}$. A weighted average lies within the range of the positive-mass positions.

Pause: A vertical forearm hangs directly below the elbow. Gravity's line passes through the elbow, so its gravitational torque about that joint is zero.

Center of Mass

Definition

The center of mass (COM) is the mass-weighted average position of all particles in a system:

Key equation 12.1
$$\;x_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum_i m_i x_i}{\sum_i m_i},\qquad y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum_i m_i y_i}{\sum_i m_i}\;$$

Why It Matters

Property Description
MotionThe COM moves as if all mass is concentrated there and all external forces act on it
Momentum$\vec{p}_{\text{total}} = M_{\text{total}} \cdot \vec{v}_{\text{cm}}$
StabilityFor a body resting on a horizontal support under gravity, the vertical projection of the COM must stay inside its support region to avoid tipping
BalanceIn a uniform gravitational field, a support through the COM gives zero gravitational torque about that support

Analogy: The Team Captain

Imagine a group of people on a raft. Even if individuals move around, the raft itself moves based on where the "average person" (the center of mass) is. The heavier people pull the average closer to them, just like heavier masses pull the COM closer.

Two-coordinate calculation / İki koordinatı ayrı hesapla. For $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $(0,0)$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $(4,2)\,\mathrm m$:

$$\begin{aligned}M&=1+3=4\,\mathrm{kg},\\x_\mathrm{cm}&=\frac{1(0)+3(4)}{4}=3.0\,\mathrm m,\\y_\mathrm{cm}&=\frac{1(0)+3(2)}{4}=1.5\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}$$

The center lies three quarters of the way toward the larger mass. TR: Aynı kütle paydasını kullanırken $x$ ve $y$ paylarını birbirine karıştırmayın. Sıfır toplam kütle için bu ortalama tanımlı değildir.

A weighted position locates the centre of mass

The centre of mass is a vector average weighted by positive masses:

$$\vec{r}_{\rm cm}=\frac{\sum_i m_i\vec{r}_i}{\sum_i m_i}.$$

Geometry. Place two masses on a light bar and draw their position arrows from one origin. The centre lies closer to the heavier mass. Translating the origin changes the reported coordinates, not the physical balance point. For two positive point masses it must lie between them; a result outside that interval is a useful error signal. The centre of mass of a hollow object can nevertheless lie in empty space.

Predict. Double both masses at unchanged positions. Then double only the right-hand mass. How does the centre move?

Interpret the weighting

Doubling both masses cancels from numerator and denominator, leaving the centre unchanged. Doubling only the right-hand mass moves the centre toward it. Coordinates alone should not be averaged unless the masses are equal.

Transfer. Positioning a robot payload, distributing circuit-board components on a carrier, and loading a process vessel all change a mass distribution. Treating its weight as acting at the centre of mass assumes an approximately uniform gravitational field.

Torque

Definition

Torque is the rotational equivalent of force. It measures how much a force tends to cause rotation:

Key equation 12.2
$$\;\vec\tau=\vec r\times\vec F,\qquad |\tau|=rF\sin\theta=F d_\perp,\qquad d_\perp=r\sin\theta\;$$

The cross product gives a vector. For planar calculations we use its signed component perpendicular to the plane: counterclockwise positive and clockwise negative.

where:

  • $r$ = distance from the pivot to where the force is applied (position-vector length)
  • $F$ = magnitude of the force
  • $\theta$ = angle between $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$
Quantity Symbol SI Unit Sign Convention
Torque $\tau$ $\mathrm{N\,m}$ CCW = positive, CW = negative
Perpendicular lever arm $d_\perp=r\sin\theta$ $\mathrm m$ A nonnegative distance
Force $F$ N Direction matters

Analogy: Opening a Door

Why is the doorknob placed at the far edge from the hinge? For a perpendicular push, $|\tau|=Fr$. A larger perpendicular distance from the hinge gives more torque for the same force. Try pushing a door near the hinge -- it barely moves!

Calculate the perpendicular distance first. A $12\,\mathrm N$ force acts $1.0\,\mathrm m$ from the pivot at $30^\circ$ to the rod:

$$d_\perp=r\sin30^\circ=0.50\,\mathrm m,\qquad |\tau|=F d_\perp=(12)(0.50)=6.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}.$$

The lever arm is measured from the pivot perpendicular to the force's line of action, not along the force arrow or vertically by default. TR: Dik uzaklığı önce şekil üzerinde işaretleyin. Kuvvetin uygulandığı nokta aynı kalsa bile yön değişince tork değişebilir.

Equilibrium needs both force balance and torque balance

Draw a rigid body and ask two separate questions: can its centre of mass accelerate, and can its rotation accelerate? Static equilibrium requires zero net force and zero net torque, together with rest. Force balance alone does not answer the turning question.

Predict. Apply two equal opposite parallel forces to opposite sides of a bar, with different lines of action. Does the bar necessarily remain at rest?

A zero sum can still turn

The net force is zero, but the two torques can add. This pair is a couple: it produces angular acceleration while the centre of mass has zero acceleration. Bring the lines of action together and the net torque becomes zero as well.

Choose a pivot to simplify the torque equation, but keep the full force balance. A support force passing through the pivot has zero torque about it; the force has not disappeared. For an unanchored object on a level support, gravity can tip it when the centre-of-mass vertical projection passes outside the support region. Anchors or other forces change that analysis.

Transfer. Check both balances when supporting a robot arm, a loaded shelf or a vessel on feet. If a calculated compressive support force is negative, reconsider whether that support remains in contact.

Static Equilibrium

Two Conditions

An object initially at rest remains in static equilibrium when both conditions hold. If it is already moving, zero net force and torque do not by themselves make it stop:

  1. Translational equilibrium (no net force): $\sum \vec{F} = 0$
  2. Rotational equilibrium (no net torque about any point): $\sum \tau = 0$
Key equation 12.3
$$\;\sum \vec F = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sum \tau = 0 \quad\text{(about any point)}\;$$

Problem-Solving Strategy

  1. Draw a free-body diagram showing all forces
  2. Choose a convenient pivot point (often where an unknown force acts, to eliminate it)
  3. Write $\sum F_x = 0$, $\sum F_y = 0$, and $\sum \tau = 0$
  4. Solve the system of equations

Key Insight

You can choose ANY point as the pivot for the torque equation. Choosing wisely (where unknown forces act) simplifies your math enormously!

Why two equations are needed. Two forces $+10\,\mathrm N$ upward at the right end and $-10\,\mathrm N$ downward at the left end of a $2.0\,\mathrm m$ bar give zero net force. About the center, each contributes $+10\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, so net torque is $+20\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The bar is not in static equilibrium.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Center of Mass of a System

Three masses are placed on the $x$-axis: $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $x=0$, $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $x=3\,\mathrm m$, and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $x=7\,\mathrm m$. Find the center of mass.

Paper solution. A center of mass is a mass-weighted average. Make a small table:

$m$ (kg) $x$ (m) $mx$ (kg m)
2 0 0
5 3 15
3 7 21
Total 10 Total 36

$$x_{\rm cm}=\frac{0+15+21}{2+5+3}=\frac{36\ \mathrm{kg\,m}}{10\ \mathrm{kg}}=\boxed{3.6\ \mathrm m}.$$ The result lies between 0 and $7\,\mathrm m$, as required for positive masses. It lies right of $3\,\mathrm m$ because the mass at $7\,\mathrm m$ has a larger rightward moment than the mass at $0\,\mathrm m$ has leftward moment about $3\,\mathrm m$.

Example 2: Beam with Two Supports

A uniform $4\,\mathrm m$ beam weighing $200\,\mathrm N$ is supported at both ends (A at $x=0$, B at $x=4\,\mathrm m$). A $500\,\mathrm N$ weight is placed at $x=1\,\mathrm m$ from end A. Find the reaction forces at A and B.

Paper solution. Draw upward reactions $R_A,R_B$. The uniform beam's own $200\,\mathrm N$ weight acts at its center, $x=2\,\mathrm m$; the $500\,\mathrm N$ load acts at $x=1\,\mathrm m$. Choose A as the torque pivot, counterclockwise positive: $$R_B(4)-200(2)-500(1)=0.$$ Add the two clockwise moments: $4R_B=400+500=900\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Divide by $4\,\mathrm m$: $R_B=225\,\mathrm N$.

Next use vertical force balance: $R_A+R_B-200-500=0$, so $R_A=700-225=475\,\mathrm N$. Check: $475+225=700\,\mathrm N$ equals the total load. Taking moments about B also gives $475(4)=200(2)+500(3)=1900\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. A carries more load because the $500\,\mathrm N$ weight is nearer A.

Engineering example: Robot Arm Moment Calculation

A simple robot arm is modelled as a single uniform horizontal segment ($L_1=0.50\,\mathrm m$, $m_1=2\,\mathrm{kg}$) holding a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ payload at its end. Calculate the torque at the shoulder joint needed to hold this position.

Principle. The segment’s weight acts at its midpoint and the payload’s weight at the tip; each torque is weight times horizontal lever arm. $$\tau_\mathrm{shoulder}=m_1g\frac{L_1}{2}+m_pgL_1 =(2)(9.81)(0.25)+(3)(9.81)(0.50)=19.6\,\mathrm{N\,m}.$$ The motor supplies counterclockwise torque to balance gravity. Worked example: Supports under a beam carrying a vessel (Chemical Engineering)

Situation. A uniform beam of length $L=4.00\,\mathrm m$ and mass $m_b=50.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on supports A (left) and B (right). A vessel of mass $m_v=300\,\mathrm{kg}$ sits $d=1.00\,\mathrm m$ from A.

Predict. Which support carries more, and does either carry more than half the total?

Symbolic. Torques about A: $R_BL=m_bg\tfrac L2+m_vgd\Rightarrow R_B=g\left(\tfrac{m_b}{2}+m_v\tfrac dL\right)$. Force balance: $R_A=(m_b+m_v)g-R_B$.

Limiting check. $d=L/2$ gives $R_A=R_B$; $d=0$ puts the whole vessel on A and leaves $R_B=m_bg/2$.

Numbers. $R_B=9.81\left(25.0+300\times\tfrac{1.00}{4.00}\right)=981\,\mathrm N$, total weight $350\times9.81=3.43\times10^3\,\mathrm N$, $R_A=3433-981=2.45\times10^3\,\mathrm N$.

Interpret. Support A carries $71\%$ of the load; the foundation under it must be specified for that share, not for half.

TÜRKÇE

Bir mesnet etrafında moment dengesi diğer mesnedin tepkisini verir; kuvvet dengesi kalanı tamamlar. Yüke yakın mesnet daha fazla taşır.

Worked example: Counterweight for a camera boom (Computer Engineering)

Situation. A light boom pivots on a bearing. A $m_c=1.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ camera sits $d_c=0.800\,\mathrm m$ from the pivot; a counterweight goes $d_w=0.250\,\mathrm m$ on the other side.

Predict. Once balanced, does the boom need a motor torque to hold a tilted angle?

Symbolic. Torque balance: $m_wgd_w=m_cgd_c\Rightarrow m_w=m_c\,d_c/d_w$. Bearing force: $F=(m_c+m_w)g$. Tilting by $\theta$ multiplies both torques by $\cos\theta$, so balance holds at every angle.

Limiting check. $d_w=d_c$ gives $m_w=m_c$; a shorter $d_w$ needs a heavier counterweight.

Numbers. $m_w=1.20\times\dfrac{0.800}{0.250}=3.84\,\mathrm{kg}$, $F=(1.20+3.84)(9.81)=49.4\,\mathrm N$.

Interpret. The tilt motor only has to overcome friction, but the bearing carries $49.4\,\mathrm N$, four times the camera's weight, because the counterweight adds to the load.

TÜRKÇE

Dengelenmiş bomda motor yalnızca sürtünmeyi yener; yatak ise kamera artı karşı ağırlığın tamamını taşır.

Worked example 12.2

Find the safe payload region on a rail

Problem. A uniform $1.20\,\mathrm m$ rail weighs $24.0\,\mathrm N$ and rests on supports at its two ends. A $60.0\,\mathrm N$ tool can be placed anywhere along it. Each support is rated for $55.0\,\mathrm N$. Find the permitted tool positions measured from the left end.

Picture and principle. Draw two upward reactions and two downward loads. Taking torque about the left end gives $R_RL=W_rL/2+W_tx$, so $R_R=W_r/2+W_tx/L$; force balance gives $R_L=W_r+W_t-R_R$. Impose both ratings.

Limiting check. At midspan, symmetry gives equal reactions. Moving the tool right increases the right reaction and decreases the left by the same amount.

Numbers and units. $$R_R=12.0\,\mathrm N+(50.0\,\mathrm{N/m})x,\quad R_L=72.0\,\mathrm N-(50.0\,\mathrm{N/m})x.$$ Requiring each to be at most $55.0\,\mathrm N$ gives $0.340\,\mathrm m\le x\le0.860\,\mathrm m$.

Interpret. The total load is below the sum of the ratings, yet an off-centre placement can overload one support.

TÜRKÇE

Toplam yük kapasitenin altında olsa da bir destek aşırı yüklenebilir. İki tepki kuvvetinin sınırını ayrı ayrı uygula.

Worked example 12.3

Balance a sensor bracket with a movable weight

Problem. A bracket and sensor together have mass $0.240\,\mathrm{kg}$, with their centre of mass $0.0350\,\mathrm m$ to the right of a pivot. An $0.0800\,\mathrm{kg}$ counterweight can slide on an arm to the left, no farther than $0.120\,\mathrm m$. Where should it be placed, and is the arm long enough?

Picture and principle. Draw both weights acting downward on opposite sides of the pivot. Zero torque requires $Mgx=mgd$, so the required leftward distance is $d=Mx/m$. The pivot reaction makes no torque about the pivot.

Limiting check. If the original centre of mass lies at the pivot, no counterweight lever arm is needed. A lighter counterweight must be farther out.

Numbers and units. $$d=\frac{(0.240\,\mathrm{kg})(0.0350\,\mathrm m)}{0.0800\,\mathrm{kg}}=0.105\,\mathrm m.$$

Interpret. The arm is long enough, leaving $0.0150\,\mathrm m$ of travel. Balance depends on mass times lever arm, not equal masses.

TÜRKÇE

Denge için kütleler değil, kütle ile moment kolunun çarpımları eşitlenir. Destek tepkisi pivot noktasına göre tork oluşturmaz.

Worked example 12.4

Position a battery to unload one support

Problem. A uniform $2.00\,\mathrm m$ platform has mass $6.00\,\mathrm{kg}$ and is supported at both ends. A $10.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ motor is fixed $0.300\,\mathrm m$ from the left end. Where should an $8.00\,\mathrm{kg}$ battery be placed to make the two support forces equal?

Picture and principle. Draw the three weights and two reactions. Equal end reactions require the combined centre of mass at $L/2$. Thus $m_pL/2+m_mx_m+m_bx_b=(m_p+m_m+m_b)L/2$; solve for $x_b$.

Limiting check. If the motor were at the centre, the battery would also have to be at the centre. Moving the motor left requires a compensating shift right.

Numbers and units. $$x_b=\frac{(6.00+10.0+8.00)(1.00)-(6.00)(1.00)-(10.0)(0.300)}{8.00}\,\mathrm m=1.88\,\mathrm m.$$ The unrounded position is $1.875\,\mathrm m$, within the platform; each reaction is $118\,\mathrm N$.

Interpret. The battery can balance the support loads, but has little remaining room before the right edge.

TÜRKÇE

Eşit destek kuvvetleri için ortak kütle merkezi orta noktada olmalıdır. Sağdaki batarya, soldaki motorun momentini dengeler.

Worked example 12.5

Find a push height that prevents tipping first

Problem. A rigid cabinet has a centred centre of mass and a base width of $0.400\,\mathrm m$ in the push direction. It is pushed horizontally, slowly, at height $h$. The static friction coefficient is $0.350$. Find the largest push height for which sliding begins no later than tipping.

Picture and principle. Draw the impending tipping pivot at the forward bottom edge. Sliding begins at $F_s=\mu_smg$; tipping begins when $F_th=mg(b/2)$, so $F_t=mgb/(2h)$. Requiring $F_s\le F_t$ gives $h\le b/(2\mu_s)$.

Limiting check. A wider base permits a higher push. With negligible friction, sliding begins before appreciable tipping torque develops.

Numbers and units. $$h_{\max}=\frac{0.400\,\mathrm m}{2(0.350)}=0.571\,\mathrm m.$$

Interpret. At the boundary the two ideal thresholds coincide. Pushing below it favours sliding first; the mass cancels from the comparison.

TÜRKÇE

Kayma ve devrilme eşiklerini ayrı bulup karşılaştır. Yüksekten itmek devrilmeyi kolaylaştırır; bu karşılaştırmada kütle sadeleşir.

Worked example 12.6

Which event happens first on a tilting tray?

Problem. A rigid rectangular block has base length $0.120\,\mathrm m$ along a slowly tilted tray, height $0.200\,\mathrm m$ normal to it, and a centred centre of mass. Its static friction coefficient is $0.800$. Predict whether it slides or tips first, and the critical angle.

Picture and principle. Draw the vertical line through the centre of mass. Sliding starts at $\tan\theta_s=\mu_s$. Tipping starts when that line reaches the downhill base edge: $(H/2)\tan\theta_t=b/2$, so $\tan\theta_t=b/H$. Compare the two angles.

Limiting check. A shorter, wider block resists tipping better. Lowering friction reduces only the sliding threshold in this ideal comparison.

Numbers and units. $$\theta_s=\tan^{-1}(0.800)=38.7^\circ,\qquad \theta_t=\tan^{-1}(0.120/0.200)=31.0^\circ.$$

Interpret. It tips first at about $31.0^\circ$. Large friction can prevent sliding yet make tipping the first loss of equilibrium.

TÜRKÇE

Sürtünmenin büyük olması her zaman daha güvenli değildir. Kayma engellenirken ağırlığın etki çizgisi tabanın dışına çıkıp devrilme başlatabilir.
