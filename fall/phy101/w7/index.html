How this week is workedBu hafta nasıl çalışılır

Every problem, worked example and experiment in this course follows the same six steps. Explain the physical meaning of the chosen law and operation before using it.

draw → choose a law → rearrange to the symbolic answer → check a limiting case → substitute with units → interpret

  1. Symbolic answer first. Isolate the unknown in symbols, before any number appears.
  2. One limiting case. Push a parameter to a value whose answer you already know — a mass to zero, an angle to $90^\circ$, a friction coefficient to zero — and confirm the formula behaves sensibly. If it does not, the algebra is wrong and you have just caught it for free, before wasting arithmetic on it.
  3. Then numbers, with units carried through every line.

Why bother: the symbolic route is less arithmetic, not more, and it is where the marks are. A correct symbolic answer with a slip in the final multiplication is nearly full credit; a correct number with no derivation is not.

For one motion problem, identify what a slope and an area measure, with units. For one force problem, name the agent of every force and the object it acts on. For one work problem, draw the angle between force and displacement and predict the sign of the work.

Diagnostic. A cart moves to the right while braking. A proposed solution gives a positive acceleration and positive net work. Can changing the cart's mass repair both signs?

Check the model before the arithmetic

With right chosen positive, its velocity decreases, so acceleration is negative. Kinetic energy decreases, so net work is negative. Changing a positive mass cannot repair the sign choices. The force diagram and displacement direction must be corrected first.

A 15-minute self-check with its worked explanation

A 4.0 kg object moving right at 2.0 m/s receives a constant net force of 8.0 N rightward over 3.0 m. Find final speed in two ways. Cover the calculation first and try it on paper.

Force and motion: $a=F_{\rm net}/m=8/4=2.0$ m/s². Time is unnecessary, so use $$v_f^2=v_i^2+2ad=2^2+2(2)(3)=16\Rightarrow v_f=4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}.$$ Work and kinetic energy: $W=8(3)=24$ J and $K_i=\tfrac12(4)(2^2)=8$ J. Then $K_f=32$ J, so $$32=\tfrac12(4)v_f^2\Rightarrow64=4v_f^2\Rightarrow v_f^2=16\Rightarrow v_f=4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}.$$ Check: both routes agree. The unit of $2ad$ is m²/s², matching $v^2$. The result is a speed; the stated force/motion directions establish that the object still moves right.
