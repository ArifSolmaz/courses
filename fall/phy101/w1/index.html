Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  1. Identify and convert between SI base units and derived units
  2. Apply dimensional analysis to verify equations and estimate quantities
  3. Use significant figures correctly in calculations
  4. Decompose vectors into components and reconstruct them
  5. Add vectors graphically and algebraically
  6. Compute dot products and vector projections
  7. Compute cross products and interpret their geometric meaning
  8. Interpret vector diagrams and use optional visualizations to check a hand calculation

Before calculating: units, signs, and a small algebra bridge

Core route (no programming required): read the idea → draw the axes → follow the hand-worked example → try a problem on paper → use a graph as a check. The Python cells are optional demonstrations. You do not need to write or understand plotting code to learn the physics.

What you need today: fractions, powers of ten, Pythagoras, sine and cosine. Keep a calculator in degree mode for angles marked °.

  1. Units cancel like factors. $72\,\mathrm{km/h}\,(1000\,\mathrm m/1\,\mathrm{km})(1\,\mathrm h/3600\,\mathrm s)=20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Multiply by ratios equal to one. The physical speed stays the same.
  2. Square the whole quantity. $(-3\,\mathrm m)^2=+9\,\mathrm{m^2}$. But $-3^2=-9$ without parentheses. Keep parentheses around negative components.
  3. Add like components. If east is $+x$ and north is $+y$, 3 m east and 4 m north give $(3,4)$ m and magnitude $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$ m. The path is 7 m; displacement magnitude is 5 m.
  4. Undo an operation on both sides. From $A_x=A\cos\theta$, divide both sides by $\cos\theta$ (if nonzero) to obtain $A=A_x/\cos\theta$. A symbol does not magically 'move across' the equals sign.

Angle safety: $\tan^{-1}(A_y/A_x)$ alone can choose the wrong quadrant. Sketch the signs first; use a reference angle or a two-argument angle calculator. A zero vector has no defined direction.

Core now / preview later: unit conversion, vector components, and vector addition are the first priorities. Dot and cross products introduce tools used again with work and torque; revisit them then rather than requiring instant mastery of every formula today.

Pause and explain: If the same displacement is written in centimetres instead of metres, does it become physically longer? No: only its numerical representation changes.

How this week is workedBu hafta nasıl çalışılır

Every problem, worked example and experiment in this course follows the same six steps. Step 4 is the one added in September 2026, and it is the one that separates a physicist from a calculator operator.

draw → choose a law → rearrange to the symbolic answer → check a limiting case → substitute with units → interpret

  1. Symbolic answer first. Isolate the unknown in symbols, before any number appears.
  2. One limiting case. Push a parameter to a value whose answer you already know — a mass to zero, an angle to $90^\circ$, a friction coefficient to zero — and confirm the formula behaves sensibly. If it does not, the algebra is wrong and you have just caught it for free, before wasting arithmetic on it.
  3. Then numbers, with units carried through every line.

Why bother: the symbolic route is less arithmetic, not more, and it is where the marks are. A correct symbolic answer with a slip in the final multiplication is nearly full credit; a correct number with no derivation is not.

SI Units & Dimensional Analysis

The SI System -- Seven Base Units

Physics describes the universe using measurable quantities. The International System of Units (SI) defines seven base units from which all other units are derived.

Quantity Unit Symbol Everyday Analogy
Length metre m A big step is about 1 m
Mass kilogram kg A bag of sugar is about 1 kg
Time second s One heartbeat is roughly 1 s
Electric current ampere A Phone charger draws ~1--2 A
Temperature kelvin K Room temp is about 295 K
Amount of substance mole mol approximately $6.022\times10^{23}$ particles
Luminous intensity candela cd A candle is about 1 cd

Dimensional Analysis

Dimensions describe the kind of quantity, independently of the chosen unit. Both metres and kilometres measure length. Write $L$ for length, $M$ for mass, and $\mathsf T$ for time; brackets mean “the dimensions of”.

$$[x]=L,\qquad [v]=L\mathsf T^{-1},\qquad [a]=L\mathsf T^{-2}.$$

You may add quantities only when they have the same dimensions. For $v=v_0+at$, check each term separately:

$$[v]=L\mathsf T^{-1},\qquad [v_0]=L\mathsf T^{-1},\qquad [at]=(L\mathsf T^{-2})(\mathsf T)=L\mathsf T^{-1}.$$

All three terms have velocity dimensions, so the equation is dimensionally consistent. The dimensions of a sum are not calculated by adding dimension symbols.

Worked contrast: $x=v_0t+\tfrac12at^2$ is consistent because $[v_0t]=L$ and $[at^2]=L$. The proposed $x=v_0t+at$ fails: its second term is a velocity, not a length. Dimensional analysis cannot determine the factor $\tfrac12$ or prove that an equation describes the correct physical situation.

Significant Figures

Significant figures tell us how precisely a number is known.

Rules for Counting Significant Figures

Rule Example Sig Figs
All non-zero digits count 324.7 4
Zeros between non-zero digits count 3007 4
Leading zeros do NOT count 0.0045 2
Trailing zeros after decimal count 2.500 4
Trailing zeros without decimal are ambiguous 1500 2, 3, or 4

Rules for Calculations

  • Multiplication/Division: result has same number of sig figs as the least precise input
  • Addition/Subtraction: result has same number of decimal places as the least precise input

Worked rounding: $2.50\times1.2=3.0$ (two significant figures); $12.11+0.3=12.4$ (one decimal place). Keep guard digits during intermediate calculations. Exact counted quantities and exact unit conversions do not limit significant figures.

Vector Components

Idea: a vector can be described by how far it points horizontally and vertically. These two signed amounts replace a single slanted arrow with two perpendicular pieces.

Choose $+x$ to the right and $+y$ upward. Measure $\theta$ counterclockwise from $+x$. Let $A=\lVert\mathbf A\rVert$ denote the nonnegative magnitude; $A_x$ and $A_y$ are signed components.

Key equation 1.1
$$\mathbf A=A_x\hat{\mathbf i}+A_y\hat{\mathbf j}$$

$$\begin{aligned} A_x&=A\cos\theta &&\text{(horizontal component)},\\ A_y&=A\sin\theta &&\text{(vertical component)}. \end{aligned}$$

Here $\hat{\mathbf i}$ and $\hat{\mathbf j}$ are unit direction vectors. Cosine gives the horizontal component because $\theta$ is measured from the horizontal axis. If the angle is measured from another axis, draw the triangle before choosing sine or cosine.

Worked example — the second quadrant: take $A=5.00\,\mathrm m$ and $\theta=130^\circ$. Predict leftward and upward components before calculating.

$$\begin{aligned} A_x&=(5.00\,\mathrm m)\cos130^\circ=(5.00)(-0.643)\,\mathrm m=-3.21\,\mathrm m,\\ A_y&=(5.00\,\mathrm m)\sin130^\circ=(5.00)(0.766)\,\mathrm m=3.83\,\mathrm m. \end{aligned}$$

Keep extra digits until the last step: $\mathbf A\approx(-3.21\hat{\mathbf i}+3.83\hat{\mathbf j})\,\mathrm m$.

To reconstruct the magnitude, square both entire components, add, and take the nonnegative square root:

$$A=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}=\sqrt{(-3.21)^2+(3.83)^2}\,\mathrm m=5.00\,\mathrm m.$$

For direction, the reference angle is $\alpha=\tan^{-1}(|A_y/A_x|)=50^\circ$. The signs locate quadrant II, so $\theta=180^\circ-\alpha=130^\circ$. A calculator's two-argument angle function can check the quadrant; coding is unnecessary. If $A_x=0$, read the direction from the vertical axis; if both components vanish, direction is undefined.

Vector Addition

Tip-to-tail: draw $\mathbf A$, then translate $\mathbf B$ without rotating or resizing it so its tail meets the tip of $\mathbf A$. The resultant $\mathbf R$ joins the starting point to the final point.

Components express the same construction algebraically:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf R&=\mathbf A+\mathbf B,\\ R_x&=A_x+B_x,\qquad R_y=A_y+B_y,\\ R&=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}. \end{aligned}$$

Worked example: walk $3\,\mathrm m$ east, then $4\,\mathrm m$ north. The vectors are $\mathbf A=(3,0)\,\mathrm m$ and $\mathbf B=(0,4)\,\mathrm m$:

$$\mathbf R=(3+0,0+4)\,\mathrm m=(3,4)\,\mathrm m,\qquad R=\sqrt{9+16}\,\mathrm m=5\,\mathrm m.$$

Both components are positive, so $\theta_R=\tan^{-1}(4/3)=53.1^\circ$ north of east. The path length is $3+4=7\,\mathrm m$; the displacement magnitude is $5\,\mathrm m$. Magnitudes add only for vectors pointing in the same direction.

Check: equal and opposite vectors give $\mathbf R=\mathbf0$. Its magnitude is zero and its direction is undefined.

Dot Product and Vector Projection

The dot product is a scalar that measures directional alignment. Let $\phi$ be the angle between two nonzero vectors, with $0^\circ\leq\phi\leq180^\circ$:

Key equation 1.2
$$\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=AB\cos\phi=A_xB_x+A_yB_y.$$

Multiply matching components, keep their signs, then add. In three dimensions also include $A_zB_z$.

Relative direction Angle Dot product
Same direction $0^\circ$ $+AB$
Perpendicular $90^\circ$ $0$
Opposite direction $180^\circ$ $-AB$

Vector Projection

For $B\ne0$, form a unit direction $\hat{\mathbf b}=\mathbf B/B$. The scalar projection of $\mathbf A$ along $\mathbf B$ is a signed number:

Key equation 1.3
$$a_{\parallel}=\mathbf A\cdot\hat{\mathbf b}=\frac{\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B}{B}=A\cos\phi.$$

Multiply this signed amount by the unit direction to obtain the vector projection:

Key equation 1.4
$$\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf B}\mathbf A=a_{\parallel}\hat{\mathbf b} =\frac{\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B}{B^2}\mathbf B.$$

Worked example: $\mathbf A=(3,4)\,\mathrm N$ and $\mathbf B=(2,0)\,\mathrm N$. First $\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=3(2)+4(0)=6\,\mathrm{N^2}$. Then $B=2\,\mathrm N$, so $a_{\parallel}=6/2=3\,\mathrm N$ and

$$\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf B}\mathbf A=\frac{6}{2^2}(2,0)\,\mathrm N=(3,0)\,\mathrm N.$$

The remaining perpendicular part is $\mathbf A-(3,0)\,\mathrm N=(0,4)\,\mathrm N$. Its dot product with $\mathbf B$ is zero, which checks the construction. With $\mathbf A=(-3,4)\,\mathrm N$, the scalar projection is $-3\,\mathrm N$: the projected vector points opposite $\mathbf B$.

Units: two forces give a dot product in $\mathrm{N^2}$, whereas the projection of a force is still in $\mathrm N$. In work, force dotted with displacement gives $\mathrm{N\,m}=\mathrm J$.

Cross Product

The cross product is a vector perpendicular to the input plane. For nonparallel nonzero vectors,

$$\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=AB\sin\phi\,\hat{\mathbf n}.$$

Choose $\hat{\mathbf n}$ with the right-hand rule: curl from $\mathbf A$ toward $\mathbf B$ through the smaller angle; your thumb gives the result's direction. Reversing the order reverses the sign:

$$\mathbf B\times\mathbf A=-(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B).$$

Parallel or opposite vectors give the zero vector, whose direction is undefined. Perpendicular inputs give the largest magnitude $AB$ for fixed $A,B$.

2D Simplification

For $\mathbf A=(A_x,A_y,0)$ and $\mathbf B=(B_x,B_y,0)$,

Key equation 1.5
$$\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=(0,0,A_xB_y-A_yB_x).$$

Worked example: $(3,1,0)\,\mathrm m\times(1,4,0)\,\mathrm m=(0,0,3\cdot4-1\cdot1)\,\mathrm{m^2}=(0,0,11)\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The positive $z$ component points out of the page. The parallelogram area is $11\,\mathrm{m^2}$; the triangle with the same edges has half that area.

3D Component Formula

$$\mathbf A\times\mathbf B= \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf i}&\hat{\mathbf j}&\hat{\mathbf k}\\ A_x&A_y&A_z\\ B_x&B_y&B_z \end{vmatrix}.$$

Expand the first row with signs plus, minus, plus:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf A\times\mathbf B={}&(A_yB_z-A_zB_y)\hat{\mathbf i}\\ &-(A_xB_z-A_zB_x)\hat{\mathbf j}\\ &+(A_xB_y-A_yB_x)\hat{\mathbf k}. \end{aligned}$$

For the middle component, distribute the minus sign: $-(A_xB_z-A_zB_x)=A_zB_x-A_xB_z$. This is the common sign-error location.

Physical Meaning

$$\text{Parallelogram area}=\lVert\mathbf A\times\mathbf B\rVert=AB\sin\phi.$$

This operation will describe torque $\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F$: only the force perpendicular to the lever arm produces turning.

TÜRKÇE

Vektörel çarpımda sonuç yeni bir vektördür. Sıra önemlidir; girdileri değiştirirsen yön ters döner. Determinantın orta teriminin önündeki eksi işaretini parantezin iki terimine de dağıt. Alan için yönü değil büyüklüğü kullan.

Worked example 1.2

Dimensional Analysis

Problem: The period $T_p$ of an ideal pendulum depends on length $\ell$ and gravitational acceleration $g$. Find the dependence by dimensional analysis. Use $\mathsf T$ for the dimension of time, distinct from the period symbol $T_p$.

1. Propose a product: $T_p=k\ell^ag^b$, where $k$ is dimensionless.

2. Substitute dimensions and combine powers:

$$[T_p]=\mathsf T,\qquad [k\ell^ag^b]=L^a(L\mathsf T^{-2})^b=L^{a+b}\mathsf T^{-2b}.$$

3. Match each exponent: the left side is $L^0\mathsf T^1$, hence

$$a+b=0,\qquad -2b=1.$$

Divide the second equation by $-2$: $b=-\tfrac12$. Substitute into the first: $a-\tfrac12=0$. Add $\tfrac12$ to both sides: $a=\tfrac12$.

4. Rewrite fractional powers as a square root:

$$T_p=k\ell^{1/2}g^{-1/2}=k\frac{\sqrt\ell}{\sqrt g}=k\sqrt{\frac\ell g}.$$

Dimensional analysis cannot determine $k$. The dynamical result for an ideal small-angle pendulum gives $k=2\pi$. For $\ell=1.00\,\mathrm m$,

$$T_p=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.00\,\mathrm m}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}}=2.01\,\mathrm s.$$

Check: $\ell/g$ has units $\mathrm{s^2}$; its square root is a time. A longer pendulum has a longer period. A period is one full back-and-forth swing, not half a swing.

TÜRKÇE

Önce üsleri eşitle; sonra iki basit denklemi çöz. Negatif üs paydaya geçer, yarım üs karekök demektir. $2\pi$ katsayısı birim analizinden gelmez. Küçük açı koşulu olmadan bu periyot formülü tam doğru değildir.

Worked example 1.3

Vector Addition

Problem: A hiker walks $5\,\mathrm{km}$ at $37^\circ$ north of east, then $3\,\mathrm{km}$ due north. Find the resultant displacement.

Solution:

Hand calculation before the optional plot

Choose east $+x$, north $+y$. All distances below are in km: $$\mathbf d_1=(5\cos37^\circ,5\sin37^\circ)=(3.99,3.01),\qquad \mathbf d_2=(0,3).$$ Add matching components: $R_x=3.99+0=3.99$ and $R_y=3.01+3=6.01$. $$R=\sqrt{(3.99)^2+(6.01)^2}=7.21\,\mathrm{km}.$$ Both components are positive, so quadrant I is correct: $$\theta=\tan^{-1}(6.01/3.99)=56.4^\circ\text{ north of east}.$$ Interpretation: displacement is about 7.21 km at 56.4° north of east. It is shorter than the 8 km path; the final position is more northward than the first leg alone.

Worked example 1.4

Dot Product and Projection

Problem: Two forces act on an object: $\mathbf F_1=(3,4)\,\mathrm N$ and $\mathbf F_2=(5,-2)\,\mathrm N$. Find:

  • (a) The dot product $\mathbf F_1\cdot\mathbf F_2$
  • (b) The angle between the forces
  • (c) The projection of $\mathbf F_1$ onto $\mathbf F_2$

Hand calculation

(a) Multiply matching components, including the negative sign: $$\mathbf F_1\cdot\mathbf F_2=3(5)+4(-2)=15-8=7\,\mathrm{N^2}.$$ (b) $F_1=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$ N and $F_2=\sqrt{5^2+(-2)^2}=\sqrt{29}$ N. From $\mathbf F_1\cdot\mathbf F_2=F_1F_2\cos\theta$, divide by $F_1F_2$, then take inverse cosine: $$\theta=\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac7{5\sqrt{29}}\right)=74.9^\circ.$$ (c) Scalar projection is $7/\sqrt{29}=1.30$ N. The vector projection is $$\operatorname{proj}_{F_2}\mathbf F_1=\frac7{29}(5,-2)=(1.21,-0.483)\,\mathrm N.$$ Check: the angle is acute because the dot product is positive. The projection points along $\mathbf F_2$ and is shorter than $\mathbf F_1$.

Worked example 1.5

Area: calculate first, round last

Problem. A rectangular surface has measured length $L=1.125\,\mathrm{m}$ and width $W=0.606\,\mathrm{m}$. Find its area and report a sensible number of significant figures. A calculator displays $0.68175$ after multiplication; decide which digits belong in the reported answer.

Picture and principle. Imagine a rectangle with one horizontal side labelled $L$ and one vertical side labelled $W$. Area counts how many unit squares cover the surface. Each length contributes one factor of metres, so the final unit is square metres. The calculation is

$$A=LW.$$

There is no unknown hidden inside another expression, so no rearrangement is required. Substitute the two measured lengths, keeping their units attached:

$$A=(1.125\,\mathrm{m})(0.606\,\mathrm{m})=0.68175\,\mathrm{m^2}.$$

For multiplication, the less precise input determines the number of significant figures. The length has four; the width has three. In $0.606$, the leading zero only locates the decimal point, while the zero between the sixes is significant. Therefore retain three significant figures in the product:

$$0.68175\longrightarrow\boxed{A=0.682\,\mathrm{m^2}}.$$

The first three significant digits are $6$, $8$, and $1$. The next digit is $7$, so the last retained digit rounds upward. Do not round each input to fewer digits before multiplying.

Meaning and check. The surface is a little more than $1.1\,\mathrm{m}$ long and about $0.6\,\mathrm{m}$ wide. An estimate $1.1\times0.6\approx0.66\,\mathrm{m^2}$ supports the answer. Reporting $0.681750000\,\mathrm{m^2}$ would add calculator digits without adding measurement information.

TÜRKÇE

Çarpma işleminde ondalık basamak sayısını değil, anlamlı rakam sayısını karşılaştırırız. $0.606$ içindeki iki altı arasındaki sıfır anlamlıdır. Önce tam çarpımı hesapla; üç anlamlı rakama yuvarlamayı en sonda yap. Birimler de çarpılır: $\mathrm{m}\times\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{m^2}$.

Worked example 1.6

Nanometres to centimetres without losing powers of ten

Problem. A wavelength near the short-wavelength end of visible light is approximately $400\,\mathrm{nm}$. Express this same physical length in centimetres. The word “approximately” matters: the conversion changes the unit, not the precision of the original estimate.

Principle. A unit-conversion fraction equals one when its numerator and denominator represent the same length. Choose its orientation so the unwanted unit cancels. Here,

$$1\,\mathrm{nm}=10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m},\qquad1\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{cm}.$$

Write the symbolic route before handling the numbers:

$$\lambda_{\mathrm{cm}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{nm}} \left(\frac{10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}}{1\,\mathrm{nm}}\right) \left(\frac{100\,\mathrm{cm}}{1\,\mathrm{m}}\right).$$

The nanometres cancel first, and the metres cancel second. Only centimetres remain. Now substitute:

$$\begin{aligned} \lambda&=(400\,\mathrm{nm})\left(\frac{10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{nm}}\right)\left(\frac{10^2\,\mathrm{cm}}{\mathrm{m}}\right)\\ &=400\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{cm}\\ &=(4\times10^2)\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{cm}\\ &=\boxed{4\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{cm}}. \end{aligned}$$

The algebra uses $10^a10^b=10^{a+b}$, so $10^2\times10^{-7}=10^{-5}$. The same answer in decimal notation is $0.00004\,\mathrm{cm}$; the wavelength itself has not changed.

Meaning and check. A nanometre is much smaller than a centimetre, so many nanometres correspond to a small numerical number of centimetres. Reverse the calculation: because $1\,\mathrm{cm}=10^7\,\mathrm{nm}$, multiplying $4\times10^{-5}$ by $10^7$ gives $4\times10^2=400$. This verifies both the exponent and the direction of conversion.

Prefixes are exact scale factors. They introduce no extra measurement uncertainty, but they also cannot recover precision missing from the original approximate wavelength.

TÜRKÇE

Dönüşüm katsayısını ezberlemek yerine birimlerin üstte ve altta birbirini götürmesini izle. Negatif üs, negatif uzunluk demek değildir; birden küçük bir sayıyı gösterir. $10^{-5}$, $1/100000$ demektir. Santimetre daha büyük bir birim olduğu için sonuçtaki sayının küçülmesi beklenir.

Worked example 1.7

Airflow: cube the length conversion

Problem. An exhaust fan has a nominal flow rate of $600\,\mathrm{ft^3/min}$, often written “600 CFM.” Convert this rate to cubic metres per second. Use $1\,\mathrm{ft}=12\,\mathrm{in}$ and $1\,\mathrm{in}=2.54\,\mathrm{cm}$. Interpret the result as a volume passing through the fan each second.

Picture and principle. Imagine a cube one foot long on every edge. Converting its volume requires converting all three edges. A single length-conversion factor is therefore insufficient:

$$1\,\mathrm{ft}=12(2.54\,\mathrm{cm})=0.3048\,\mathrm{m},$$

$$1\,\mathrm{ft^3}=(0.3048\,\mathrm{m})^3=0.028316846592\,\mathrm{m^3}.$$

For a rate, convert the denominator as well. Since one minute contains sixty seconds, the volume passing in one second is one-sixtieth of the volume passing in one minute. The complete symbolic conversion is

$$Q_{\mathrm{SI}}=Q_{\mathrm{CFM}}\left(\frac{0.3048\,\mathrm{m}}{1\,\mathrm{ft}}\right)^3\left(\frac{1\,\mathrm{min}}{60\,\mathrm{s}}\right).$$

Substitution makes the cancellation visible:

$$Q=\left(600\frac{\mathrm{ft^3}}{\mathrm{min}}\right)\left(\frac{0.3048\,\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{ft}}\right)^3\left(\frac{\mathrm{min}}{60\,\mathrm{s}}\right)=0.283168\ldots\,\mathrm{m^3/s}.$$

Thus the nominal converted rate is approximately

$$\boxed{Q\approx0.283\,\mathrm{m^3/s}}.$$

Meaning and check. Each second the fan moves roughly $0.28\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of air. A quick estimate uses $0.3^3\approx0.027$ and $600/60=10$, producing about $0.27\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. This agrees with the more careful conversion. The extra calculator digits above describe the arithmetic; they do not establish the fan's measurement accuracy.

As a second check, multiplying the SI result by sixty gives the volume per minute; dividing that by one cubic foot recovers the original rating.

TÜRKÇE

Hacim dönüşümünde yalnızca bir kenarı çevirmiyoruz. Üç kenar da $0.3048$ ile çarpıldığı için katsayının küpü alınır. “Dakikada” verilen debiyi “saniyede” yazarken ayrıca $60$'a böleriz. Birim iptali iki işlemi de kontrol eder; sayıya bakarak karar vermekten daha güvenilirdir.

Worked example 1.8

Three flight legs, one final displacement

Problem. An airplane first flies $66\,\mathrm{km}$ at $30^\circ$ east of north, then $49\,\mathrm{km}$ south, then $100\,\mathrm{km}$ at $30^\circ$ north of west. Find its distance from its starting point. The task concerns final displacement, not the total length of the route.

Picture and principle. Draw east as $+x$ and north as $+y$. Place the three displacement arrows tip to tail. Their vector sum joins the original starting point to the final endpoint. Add the horizontal components separately from the vertical components:

$$R_x=A_x+B_x+C_x,\qquad R_y=A_y+B_y+C_y.$$

For the first leg, the angle is measured from north, so the northward component uses cosine. For the last leg, westward motion makes its $x$ component negative:

$$\begin{aligned} A_x&=66\sin30^\circ=33.0\,\mathrm{km},&A_y&=66\cos30^\circ=57.2\,\mathrm{km},\\ B_x&=0,&B_y&=-49\,\mathrm{km},\\ C_x&=-100\cos30^\circ=-86.6\,\mathrm{km},&C_y&=100\sin30^\circ=50.0\,\mathrm{km}. \end{aligned}$$

Therefore,

$$R_x=-53.6\,\mathrm{km},\qquad R_y=58.2\,\mathrm{km}.$$

Reconstruct the magnitude from these perpendicular components:

$$R=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}=\sqrt{(-53.6\,\mathrm{km})^2+(58.2\,\mathrm{km})^2}=79.1\ldots\,\mathrm{km}.$$

The airplane ends approximately $\boxed{79\,\mathrm{km}}$ from its starting point. Its endpoint is northwest of the start because $R_x<0$ and $R_y>0$.

Meaning and check. The aircraft actually travels $66+49+100=215\,\mathrm{km}$. Its straight-line displacement is smaller, as expected when the route changes direction. Each component is also smaller than the magnitude, which checks the square-root calculation.

TÜRKÇE

“Doğunun kuzeyi” ile “kuzeyin doğusu” aynı açı referansı değildir. Açıyı hangi eksenden ölçtüğünü önce çiz. İşareti trigonometrik işlemden önce yön belirler: batı ve güney negatiftir. Toplam yol için uzunlukları, yer değiştirme için bileşenleri toplarız.

Worked example 1.9

The angle between two box diagonals

Problem. A rectangular box has vertices $A=(0,0,0)$, $G=(2.00,4.00,3.00)$ and $H=(0,4.00,3.00)$, with all coordinates expressed in one common length unit. Find the angle between segments $AG$ and $AH$. The nonzero coordinates have three significant figures. The remaining box vertices are not needed for this angle.

Picture and principle. Imagine both segments starting at corner $A$. Segment $AH$ lies on a rectangular face; segment $AG$ reaches the opposite corner through the box. Form direction vectors by subtracting the starting coordinates from the ending coordinates:

$$\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{r}_G-\mathbf{r}_A=(2,4,3),\qquad\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{r}_H-\mathbf{r}_A=(0,4,3).$$

The dot product connects component arithmetic to the geometric angle:

$$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=|\mathbf{u}|\,|\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta.$$

Divide both sides by the positive product of magnitudes, then apply inverse cosine:

$$\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{u}|\,|\mathbf{v}|}\right).$$

Calculate each ingredient before substituting:

$$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=2(0)+4(4)+3(3)=25,$$

$$|\mathbf{u}|=\sqrt{2^2+4^2+3^2}=\sqrt{29},\qquad|\mathbf{v}|=\sqrt{0^2+4^2+3^2}=5.$$

Both numerator and denominator carry the square of the common length unit, so the ratio is dimensionless:

$$\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{25}{5\sqrt{29}}\right)=\boxed{21.8^\circ}.$$

Meaning and check. The positive dot product implies an acute angle. Geometrically, adding a $2$-unit perpendicular offset to a $5$-unit face diagonal gives an independent check: $\tan\theta=2/5$, also yielding $21.8^\circ$. The answer is independent of whether the common length unit is metres or centimetres.

TÜRKÇE

Noktaların koordinatlarıyla doğrultu vektörlerini ayır: genel durumda bitişten başlangıcı çıkarmalısın. Burada başlangıç orijin olduğu için sayılar değişmiyor. $\cos^{-1}$ ters kosinüs fonksiyonudur; $1/\cos$ anlamına gelmez. Hesap makinesinin derece modunda olduğundan emin ol.

Write the components of a vector and explain why displacement need not equal path length.

Write one sentence naming the physical principle and one line of algebra you can now explain. TR: Sonuca nasıl ulaştığını bir cümleyle anlat; emin olmadığın ilk adımı işaretle.

Model responses / Örnek yanıtlar — open after trying

Entry: “A distance of 5 m” identifies a quantity, number and unit. A number such as 5 alone cannot identify the physical distance.

Exit: For magnitude $A$ at angle $\theta$ from positive $x$, $A_x=A\cos\theta$ and $A_y=A\sin\theta$. Walking 3 m out and 3 m back gives distance 6 m but displacement 0 m: displacement compares endpoints.

Visual prediction: At $130^\circ$, a magnitude of 5 gives $A_x=5\cos130^\circ=-3.21$ and $A_y=5\sin130^\circ=3.83$. The signs locate quadrant II. Squaring and adding gives $A_x^2+A_y^2=25$ (before rounding), so the magnitude stays 5. Rotating changes components, not length. In the addition view, equal opposite vectors give a zero resultant with undefined direction. In projection, the signed component is positive at $0^\circ$, zero at $90^\circ$, and negative at $180^\circ$.
