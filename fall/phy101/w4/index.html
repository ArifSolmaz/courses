4.1Before you startBaşlamadan önce

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  1. State Newton's three laws of motion and explain their physical meaning
  2. Draw correct free body diagrams (FBDs) for objects on flat and inclined surfaces
  3. Distinguish between static and kinetic friction and apply $\lvert f_s\rvert\le\mu_sN$ at rest and $\lvert f_k\rvert=\mu_kN$ during sliding
  4. Solve for acceleration, tension, and normal forces in single-body and two-body problems
  5. Simulate motion on an inclined plane and in Atwood machine configurations

Before calculating: a free-body diagram becomes algebra

Core route: select one object → draw only forces acting on it → choose axes → write $\sum F_x=ma_x$ and $\sum F_y=ma_y$ → solve by hand. The simulations are optional checks; you are learning mechanics, not Python syntax.

Forces are interactions. Weight $mg$ comes from Earth, the normal force $N$ from a surface, tension $T$ from a string, and friction from contact. 'Net force' is their sum, not an extra arrow. A third-law partner acts on the other object and does not belong in the same free-body diagram.

Algebra bridge: for a lifted mass, take up positive. Isolate tension $T$:

$$\begin{aligned} T-mg&=ma,\\ T&=ma+mg &&\text{add }mg\text{ to both sides},\\ T&=m(a+g) &&\text{factor the common mass }m. \end{aligned}$$

Both terms in the parentheses have acceleration units. Thus $\mathrm{kg}\times\mathrm{m/s^2}=\mathrm N$. If $a=0$, the expression reduces to $T=mg$.

Inclines: choose one axis along the slope and one perpendicular. Weight components are $mg\sin\theta$ down the slope and $mg\cos\theta$ into it. If no other perpendicular force acts and there is no perpendicular acceleration, $N=mg\cos\theta$. On a horizontal surface with an angled push, $N$ need not equal $mg$.

Friction decision: while at rest, $0\le\lvert f_s\rvert\le\mu_sN$; static friction adjusts to what is needed. Check that limit before assuming motion. During sliding, $\lvert f_k\rvert=\mu_kN$ opposite relative sliding. Knowing only $\mu_k$ is insufficient to decide whether a resting box starts moving.

Review stop: a block moving right at constant speed has zero net horizontal force. It can still have two nonzero opposing horizontal forces. Draw them and show their cancellation.

First 15 minutes: repair Week 03İlk 15 dakika: 3. haftayı onar

Week 03 carried two topics in one session, so this week opens by repairing the part that matters most for everything that follows: drawing one object and every force on it. Work through the three checks below before the new material. If all three are comfortable, you have lost nothing; if one is not, you have just saved the rest of the course.

Check 1 — what belongs on the diagram? A block is sliding to the right along a rough horizontal table, having been pushed a moment ago and now released. List every force acting on the block.

Answer / Yanıt

Three forces: weight $mg$ down, normal force $N$ up from the table, friction $f$ pointing left, opposing the motion.

There is no forward force. The push has finished; nothing is touching the block from behind. The most common error in mechanics is drawing a forward arrow because the block is moving forward — motion is not a force. Velocity and force are different arrows, and they are often in different directions.

TÜRKÇE

Üç kuvvet: ağırlık, normal kuvvet ve harekete karşı sola doğru sürtünme. İleri doğru kuvvet yoktur: itme bitmiştir. Hareket bir kuvvet değildir; hız ve kuvvet farklı oklardır.

Check 2 — turn the diagram into algebra. A block on a frictionless incline of angle $\theta$. Resolve the weight into components parallel and perpendicular to the surface, and write the two equations.

Answer / Yanıt

Choose axes along and perpendicular to the slope, not horizontal and vertical — that single choice is what makes incline problems easy.

Key equation 4.1
$$\;\text{along: } a = g\sin\theta, \qquad \text{perpendicular: } N = mg\cos\theta\;$$

Limiting checks: $\theta \to 0$ gives $a \to 0$ and $N \to mg$ (flat table ✅); $\theta \to 90^\circ$ gives $a \to g$ and $N \to 0$ (free fall ✅). Note the mass cancels from $a$ — and $\sin$ goes with the direction of motion while $\cos$ goes with the normal. Swapping those two is the second most common error of the semester.

TÜRKÇE

Eksenleri yatay/düşey değil, eğime paralel ve dik seç; eğik düzlem problemlerini kolaylaştıran tek şey bu seçimdir. Sınır kontrolleri formülü doğrular. $\sin$ hareket yönüyle, $\cos$ normalle gider; bunları karıştırmak dönemin en sık ikinci hatasıdır.

Check 3 — Newton's third law, precisely. A book rests on a table. The book's weight is $mg$ down and the table pushes up with $N = mg$. Are these two forces a third-law pair?

Answer / Yanıt

No. A third-law pair acts on two different objects, and these both act on the book.

  • Weight: the Earth pulls the book down. Its partner is the book pulling the Earth up.
  • Normal force: the table pushes the book up. Its partner is the book pushing the table down.

These two forces on the book happen to be equal here only because the book is in equilibrium. Put the book in a lift that is accelerating upward and $N > mg$ — yet each force still has exactly the same third-law partner. Equal and opposite on the same object is equilibrium; equal and opposite on two objects is the third law. Keeping those apart is worth more marks than any formula this week.

TÜRKÇE

Hayır. Üçüncü yasa çifti iki farklı cisme etki eder; bunların ikisi de kitaba etki ediyor. Ağırlığın eşi, kitabın Yer'i yukarı çekmesidir; normal kuvvetin eşi, kitabın masayı aşağı itmesidir. Burada eşit olmaları yalnızca kitap dengede olduğu içindir: yukarı ivmelenen bir asansörde $N > mg$ olur, ama eşler değişmez. Aynı cisimde eşit ve zıt → denge; iki cisimde eşit ve zıt → üçüncü yasa.

How this week is workedBu hafta nasıl çalışılır

Every problem, worked example and experiment in this course follows the same six steps. Step 4 is the one added in September 2026, and it is the one that separates a physicist from a calculator operator.

draw → choose a law → rearrange to the symbolic answer → check a limiting case → substitute with units → interpret

  1. Symbolic answer first. Isolate the unknown in symbols, before any number appears.
  2. One limiting case. Push a parameter to a value whose answer you already know — a mass to zero, an angle to $90^\circ$, a friction coefficient to zero — and confirm the formula behaves sensibly. If it does not, the algebra is wrong and you have just caught it for free, before wasting arithmetic on it.
  3. Then numbers, with units carried through every line.

Why bother: the symbolic route is less arithmetic, not more, and it is where the marks are. A correct symbolic answer with a slip in the final multiplication is nearly full credit; a correct number with no derivation is not.

Common forces, the friction model and a solving strategyKuvvetler, sürtünme modeli ve çözüm stratejisi

ŞEKİL 4.1

Uygulanan kuvvete karşı sürtünme. Kayma yokken statik sürtünme, μs N değil, gereken değerdir — 45°'lik doğru. μs N yalnızca ulaşabileceği en büyük değerdir. Kayma başlayınca kuvvet sabit kinetik değere düşer ve ne kadar çekerseniz çekin orada kalır.

Common Forces

Force Symbol Direction Magnitude or condition
Weight $\vec W$ Vertically down $W=mg$
Normal $\vec N$ Perpendicular to contact surface Determined by the perpendicular force equation; $N\ge0$ for ordinary contact
Static friction $\vec f_s$ Along contact, opposing relative slipping tendency $0\le \lvert f_s\rvert\le\mu_sN$
Kinetic friction $\vec f_k$ Opposite relative sliding at the contact $\lvert f_k\rvert=\mu_kN$
Tension $\vec T$ Along a taut string, pulling Equal along an ideal massless string over an ideal pulley
Applied force $\vec F_{\rm app}$ Direction stated in the problem Value stated in the problem

Small force balance: A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ box is pushed downward by an additional vertical $5\,\mathrm N$ while resting on a floor. Upward positive gives

$$N-mg-5\,\mathrm N=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad N=(2)(9.81)+5=24.6\,\mathrm N.$$

Adding $mg+5\,\mathrm N$ to both sides isolates $N$. It exceeds the weight because the surface supports the extra push too.

TÜRKÇE

Normal kuvvetini otomatik olarak $mg$ yazma. Düşey ivme sıfır olsa bile ek aşağı kuvvet, zeminin daha büyük tepki kuvveti vermesini gerektirir.

Friction Model

Key equation 4.2
$$\;0\le f_s\le\mu_s N\quad\text{(static, adjusts)},\qquad f_k=\mu_k N\quad\text{(kinetic, fixed)}\;$$

Static friction adjusts to the needed value until its limit. At equality the contact is at impending slip; a larger demand cannot be supported by static friction. During sliding use the kinetic magnitude and the sliding direction.

Think–Pair–Explain: A horizontal contact has $N=20\,\mathrm N$, $\mu_s=0.40$, and $\mu_k=0.30$. A resting box is pulled with $5\,\mathrm N$. What is friction? What changes for a $10\,\mathrm N$ pull?

Model response: The static limit is $(0.40)(20)=8\,\mathrm N$. At a $5\,\mathrm N$ pull, static friction is $5\,\mathrm N$, not $8\,\mathrm N$. A $10\,\mathrm N$ pull exceeds the limit; once sliding begins, kinetic friction is $(0.30)(20)=6\,\mathrm N$, giving net force $10-6=4\,\mathrm N$.

TÜRKÇE

$\mu_sN$ her durumda sürtünmenin kendisi değildir, sağlayabileceği en büyük değerdir. Kayma başladıktan sonra farklı bir model ve $\mu_k$ kullanılır.

Strategy for Solving Force Problems

  1. Select an object and identify its contacts.
  2. Draw only the real forces acting on it; net force is their sum, not an extra arrow.
  3. Choose axes; tilt them with an incline when useful.
  4. Write $\sum F_x=ma_x$ and $\sum F_y=ma_y$ with signs.
  5. Rearrange symbolically, substitute units, then test direction and limiting cases.

Model answer / Örnek yanıt — open after trying

First calculate the available static-friction limit; then compare the demand:

$$\begin{aligned} f_{s,\max}&=\mu_sN=(0.40)(20\,\mathrm N)=8\,\mathrm N,\\ f_s&=5\,\mathrm N\quad\text{opposite the pull},\\ \sum F_x&=5-5=0\,\mathrm N. \end{aligned}$$

The required $5\,\mathrm N$ is below the limit. A demand greater than $8\,\mathrm N$ cannot be balanced. Türkçe: Sınır ile gerçekleşen kuvvet farklıdır. Sürtünme sekiz newton olsaydı net kuvvet sıfır olmazdı; durgunluk koşuluyla çelişirdi.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Block on a Flat Surface

A $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ block on a horizontal surface ($\mu_k=0.25$) is pushed with $40\,\mathrm N$. Find the acceleration.

Hand calculation and the missing assumption

Assume the block is already sliding in the direction of the horizontal push. Only $\mu_k$ is supplied, so we cannot determine whether a resting block starts moving.

Choose forward and upward positive. Vertical acceleration is zero. First find normal force $N$, then kinetic-friction magnitude $f_k$:

$$\begin{aligned} N-mg&=0,\\ N&=mg=(10\,\mathrm{kg})(9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2})=98.1\,\mathrm N,\\ f_k&=\mu_kN=(0.25)(98.1\,\mathrm N)=24.5\,\mathrm N. \end{aligned}$$

Friction points backward. Write its minus sign in the horizontal sum, then divide both sides by mass:

$$\begin{aligned} F-f_k&=ma,\\ 40\,\mathrm N-24.5\,\mathrm N&=(10\,\mathrm{kg})a,\\ a&=\frac{15.5\,\mathrm N}{10\,\mathrm{kg}}=1.55\,\mathrm{m/s^2}. \end{aligned}$$

Result: Acceleration is $1.55\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ forward under the sliding assumption.

Check: It is smaller than the frictionless value $F/m=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The net force, not the applied force alone, equals $ma$.

TÜRKÇE

Önce düşey dengeyle normal kuvveti bul; sürtünme bu kuvvete bağlıdır. Ardından yatay kuvvetleri işaretleriyle topla. Kinetik katsayı kayma hesabına yeter, fakat başlangıçta durgun kutunun harekete geçip geçmeyeceğini belirlemez.

Worked example 4.1

Example 2: Block on an Incline

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ block is placed on a $30^\circ$ incline with $\mu_s=0.4$ and $\mu_k=0.3$. Does it slide? If yes, what is its acceleration?

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Hand calculation: first decide whether it moves

Choose downhill positive. Let $W_{\parallel}$ be the downhill weight component. There is no acceleration perpendicular to the plane:

$$\begin{aligned} N&=mg\cos30^\circ=(5)(9.81)\cos30^\circ=42.5\,\mathrm N,\\ W_{\parallel}&=mg\sin30^\circ=(5)(9.81)\sin30^\circ=24.5\,\mathrm N,\\ f_{s,\max}&=\mu_sN=(0.4)(42.5)=17.0\,\mathrm N. \end{aligned}$$

The needed uphill friction exceeds the static limit: $24.5\,\mathrm N>17.0\,\mathrm N$. Therefore the block slides downhill.

After sliding starts, replace static friction by kinetic friction. Keep its uphill direction as a minus sign:

$$\begin{aligned} f_k&=\mu_kN=(0.3)(42.5)=12.7\,\mathrm N,\\ W_{\parallel}-f_k&=ma,\\ a&=\frac{24.5\,\mathrm N-12.7\,\mathrm N}{5\,\mathrm{kg}}=2.36\,\mathrm{m/s^2}. \end{aligned}$$

The same result follows symbolically by factoring and dividing by nonzero mass:

$$m\bigl(g\sin\theta-\mu_kg\cos\theta\bigr)=ma$$

$$\boxed{\;a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)\;}$$

Check: The acceleration lies between zero and the frictionless value $g\sin30^\circ=4.91\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The critical static angle satisfies $\tan\theta_c=\mu_s$, and $30^\circ>\tan^{-1}(0.4)$.

TÜRKÇE

Kayma kararını kuvvetlerin büyüklüklerini karşılaştırarak ver. Kararda $\mu_s$, hareket başladıktan sonraki ivmede $\mu_k$ kullanılır. Son bölümde her terimde aynı kütle bulunduğu için denklemin iki tarafını kütleye bölüyoruz.

ŞEKİL 4.2

Ağırlığın 30°'lik eğime paralel ve dik bileşenleri. Eksenleri yatay/düşey değil yüzey boyunca seçmek, eğik düzlem problemlerini kolaylaştıran tek karardır: hareketi yalnızca mg sin θ sürer, yüzeyin taşıması gereken ise mg cos θ'dır.

Worked example 4.2

Example 3: Atwood Machine

Two masses $m_1=7\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2=3\,\mathrm{kg}$ are connected by a string over a frictionless pulley. Find acceleration and tension.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Hand calculation: add two force equations

Assume a light, inextensible string and an ideal pulley. Take downward positive for $m_1$ and upward positive for $m_2$. Their acceleration magnitudes are the same, denoted $a$; the string tension is $T$ on each side.

$$\begin{aligned} m_1g-T&=m_1a &&\text{heavier mass},\\ T-m_2g&=m_2a &&\text{lighter mass}. \end{aligned}$$

Add the equations. The opposite tension terms cancel:

$$\begin{aligned} (m_1-m_2)g&=(m_1+m_2)a,\\ a&=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}g,\\ &=\frac{7-3}{7+3}(9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2})=3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}. \end{aligned}$$

To find tension, use the lighter mass and add its weight to both sides:

$$\begin{aligned} T-m_2g&=m_2a,\\ T&=m_2(g+a),\\ &=(3\,\mathrm{kg})(9.81+3.92)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=41.2\,\mathrm N. \end{aligned}$$

Check: The heavier mass gives the same answer, $T=m_1(g-a)=(7)(9.81-3.92)=41.2\,\mathrm N$. Acceleration is less than $g$; tension lies between the two weights.

TÜRKÇE

İki kütle için pozitif yönleri farklı seçiyoruz, çünkü biri aşağı inerken diğeri yukarı çıkar. Bu seçimle aynı pozitif $a$ büyüklüğü kullanılabilir. Denklemleri toplarken gerilme yok olur; gerilmeyi daha sonra tek kütlenin denkleminden geri buluruz.

A descending elevator can make the scale read more

Problem. A $60.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ person stands on a scale inside an elevator. The elevator is moving downward but slowing at $2.00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the scale reading in newtons, using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Separate velocity from acceleration. Moving downward tells us the velocity direction. Slowing downward motion requires an upward acceleration. Choose upward as positive, so the correct acceleration is $a_y=+2.00\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The scale measures the upward normal contact force $N$ on the person's feet; it does not directly measure the gravitational force.

For the person, the forces are $N$ upward and $mg$ downward. Newton's second law gives

$$N-mg=ma_y.$$

Rearrange the force equation. Add $mg$ to both sides and factor out the common mass:

$$\begin{aligned} N&=ma_y+mg,\\ N&=m(a_y+g)\\ &=(60.0\,\mathrm{kg})(2.00+9.81)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\ &=\boxed{709\,\mathrm N}. \end{aligned}$$

The person's ordinary weight is only

$$mg=(60.0)(9.81)\,\mathrm N=589\,\mathrm N.$$

Interpret the difference. The additional $709-589=120\,\mathrm N$ provides the upward net force that reduces the downward speed. The person can feel heavier while travelling downward; acceleration determines the change in apparent weight. If the scale displays a mass-like reading, its calibrated display would be $N/g\approx72.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, although the person's actual mass remains $60.0\,\mathrm{kg}$.

Sense check. A normal force below the weight would give downward acceleration, making the elevator speed up downward. That would contradict the stated slowing motion. Our larger normal force has the required sign.

TÜRKÇE

“Aşağı gidiyor” ile “aşağı ivmeleniyor” aynı değildir. Aşağı yönlü hız azalırken ivme yukarı yönlüdür. Baskülün gösterdiği kuvvet $N$'dir; kişinin kütlesi değişmez. İşaret seçimini önce kelimelerle belirlemek, formülde yanlışlıkla $g-a$ kullanmayı önler.

Up and back on a frictionless incline

Problem. A brief push gives a block an initial speed of $12.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ up a frictionless incline at $20.0^\circ$ above horizontal. Once the push ends, find its distance up the slope before stopping and the total time until it returns to its starting point. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Choose the slope as the axis. Positive displacement points uphill. The normal force cancels the perpendicular component of weight, while gravity's parallel component points downhill. After the brief push, there is no continuing applied force. Thus

$$ma=-mg\sin\theta\quad\Rightarrow\quad a=-g\sin\theta=-3.35\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.$$

The mass cancels because it multiplies both sides. This acceleration remains downhill throughout the motion, including at the instant the block stops.

Find the turning-point distance. At the top, $v=0$. Rearrange the equation without time:

$$\begin{aligned} 0&=v_0^2+2as,\\ s&=-\frac{v_0^2}{2a} =\frac{v_0^2}{2g\sin\theta}\\ &=\frac{(12.0)^2}{2(9.81)\sin20.0^\circ}\,\mathrm m =\boxed{21.5\,\mathrm m}. \end{aligned}$$

Find the complete travel time. From $v=v_0+at$, the uphill time is $t_{\rm up}=v_0/(g\sin\theta)=3.58\,\mathrm s$. Returning to the original position requires the nonzero solution of

$$0=v_0t-\frac12g\sin\theta\,t^2 =t\left(v_0-\frac12g\sin\theta\,t\right).$$

The factor $t=0$ is the initial instant. The return occurs at

$$t_{\rm return}=\frac{2v_0}{g\sin\theta}=\boxed{7.15\,\mathrm s}.$$

Sense check. The return time is twice the ascent time, as expected for this frictionless constant-acceleration motion. The final velocity is $-12.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$: equal speed, opposite direction.

TÜRKÇE

En üst noktada hız sıfırdır ama ivme sıfır değildir; yerçekimi etkisini sürdürür. Dönüş zamanı denkleminde iki kök bulunur. $t=0$ başlangıcı, diğer kök ise bloğun gerçekten geri döndüğü anı gösterir.

Source: 05_testbank.doc, Section 5.2 Problems, Question 12. Original numerical givens retained; restated as an open worked example.

Static friction adjusts; kinetic friction has a different value

Problem. A $50.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ box rests on a horizontal floor. Its friction coefficients are $\mu_s=0.300$ and $\mu_k=0.200$. Determine the friction force when the box is subjected separately to horizontal pushes of $140\,\mathrm N$ and $175\,\mathrm N$. Start each case from rest and use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

First determine whether motion starts. The push is horizontal, so it does not change vertical force balance:

$$N=mg=(50.0)(9.81)\,\mathrm N=491\,\mathrm N.$$

Static friction can take any required magnitude up to a maximum:

$$0\le f_s\le\mu_sN,\qquad f_{s,\max}=(0.300)(491)=147\,\mathrm N.$$

The expression $\mu_sN$ is a limit, not the friction force in every stationary situation. Compare the push with this limit before choosing the friction model.

Case (a): the push is $140\,\mathrm N$. Static friction can match this because $140<147$. The horizontal force equation for a stationary box is

$$F-f_s=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad f_s=F=\boxed{140\,\mathrm N}.$$

It acts opposite the push. There is no acceleration and no sliding.

Case (b): the push is $175\,\mathrm N$. Static friction cannot supply $175\,\mathrm N$, so the box starts sliding. Once it slides, use the kinetic coefficient:

$$f_k=\mu_kN=(0.200)(491)=\boxed{98.1\,\mathrm N}.$$

The resulting horizontal net force is $175-98.1=76.9\,\mathrm N$, giving $a=76.9/50.0=1.54\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ while this push continues.

Sense check. At a zero push, static friction would be zero, even though $\mu_sN$ is still $147\,\mathrm N$. This limiting example shows why automatically substituting $f_s=\mu_sN$ is incorrect.

TÜRKÇE

Statik sürtünme, cismi durgun tutmak için gereken değere ayarlanır; $147\,\mathrm N$ onun ulaşabileceği en büyük değerdir. Eşik aşılınca hesapta $\mu_s$ yerine $\mu_k$ kullanılır. İki durum bağımsızdır; ikinci durumu birincinin devamı olarak yorumlamıyoruz.

Source: 05_testbank.doc, Section 5.2 Problems, Question 15. Original numerical givens retained; restated as an open worked example.

Friction accelerates an object onto a moving belt

Problem. A $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ computer is placed on a conveyor belt moving at $3.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Initially its horizontal speed relative to the floor is zero. The coefficients are $\mu_s=0.50$ and $\mu_k=0.30$. Find its horizontal travel distance before it moves together with the belt. Model the motion after landing; use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Decide the friction direction. At first the belt slides forward underneath the computer. Friction opposes this relative sliding, so the belt's frictional force on the computer points forward. Friction can therefore increase an object's speed; it does not always point opposite that object's velocity relative to the room.

During sliding the normal force is $N=mg$. Newton's second law gives

$$\begin{aligned} f_k&=\mu_kmg=ma,\\ a&=\mu_kg=(0.30)(9.81)=2.94\,\mathrm{m/s^2}. \end{aligned}$$

We cancel the same nonzero mass from both sides. The computer accelerates until its floor-frame speed reaches the belt speed. At that point the surfaces no longer slide relative to each other.

Calculate the distance. With $v_0=0$ and $v=3.1\,\mathrm{m/s}$,

$$\begin{aligned} v^2-v_0^2&=2as,\\ s&=\frac{v^2}{2a} =\frac{(3.1\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2(2.94\,\mathrm{m/s^2})}\\ &=\boxed{1.63\,\mathrm m}. \end{aligned}$$

Check with time. The matching time is $t=v/a=1.05\,\mathrm s$. The computer's average speed during constant acceleration is $(0+3.1)/2=1.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$; multiplying by the unrounded time reproduces $1.63\,\mathrm m$. After matching speeds on an ideal horizontal belt, no horizontal force is needed to maintain that constant speed.

TÜRKÇE

Sürtünmenin yönünü zemine göre hıza bakarak seçme; temas eden yüzeylerin birbirine göre kaymasına bak. Başlangıçta kayma olduğu için $\mu_k$ gerekir. Verilen $\mu_s$, bu kayma evresinin hesabına girmez. Son hızın kayış hızına eşitlenmesi, hangi anda hesabı durduracağımızı belirler.

Source: 05_testbank.doc, Section 5.2 Problems, Question 23. Numerical givens retained; horizontal start and post-landing model stated explicitly.

Resolve gravity and friction on a downhill slope

Problem. A $6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ box slides down a slope inclined at $39^\circ$ above horizontal. The kinetic friction coefficient is $0.19$. Find its acceleration down the slope, using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Rotate the coordinate axes to match the surface. Let positive $x$ point downhill and positive $y$ point perpendicular to the surface, away from it. Weight has a downhill component $mg\sin\theta$ and a perpendicular component $mg\cos\theta$ into the surface. Friction acts uphill because the box is already sliding downhill.

There is no acceleration through the surface. Therefore

$$N-mg\cos\theta=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad N=mg\cos\theta.$$

This is why the normal force on an incline is generally smaller than $mg$. The kinetic friction magnitude is

$$f_k=\mu_kN=\mu_kmg\cos\theta.$$

Write and rearrange the downhill equation. Keeping directions explicit,

$$\begin{aligned} mg\sin\theta-f_k&=ma,\\ mg\sin\theta-\mu_kmg\cos\theta&=ma,\\ a&=g\left(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta\right). \end{aligned}$$

Substitute the angle in degrees, taking care that both trigonometric terms use the same angle:

$$a=(9.81)\left[\sin39^\circ-(0.19)\cos39^\circ\right] =\boxed{4.73\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\ \text{downhill}}.$$

Interpret and check. Without friction the acceleration would be $g\sin39^\circ\approx6.17\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Friction reduces it, as our smaller answer shows. The result is still positive, so the already sliding box speeds up downhill. The mass cancels from this ideal model, although the individual normal and friction forces both depend on mass.

TÜRKÇE

Normal kuvveti önce dik doğrultudaki dengeden bul. Sonra sürtünmeyi $\mu_kN$ ile hesapla; doğrudan $\mu_kmg$ kullanmak eğimin etkisini kaybettirir. Eksi işareti sürtünmenin seçtiğimiz pozitif yöne, yani yokuş aşağıya, karşı olmasından gelir. Ortak $m$ çarpanı ancak bütün terimlerde bulunduğu için sadeleşir.
