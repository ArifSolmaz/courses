Section 4.1

Force and interactions

What force means in physics, why forces are vectors, and what the net force on an object is.

A force is a push or a pull. More precisely, it is an interaction between two objects, or between an object and its environment. That is why we always speak of the force that one object exerts on a second object. Force is a vector: it has a magnitude and a direction. The SI unit of magnitude is the newton (N).

Contact forces need touching. Long-range forces (gravity, magnetism) act across empty space. The gravitational force the earth exerts on you is your weight.

TÜRKÇE

Kuvvet bir itme ya da çekmedir; daha kesin olarak iki cisim arasındaki bir etkileşimdir. Kuvvet vektördür: büyüklüğü ve yönü vardır, SI birimi newton (N)’dur. Temas kuvvetleri (normal kuvvet n, sürtünme kuvveti f, gerilme kuvveti T) dokunma gerektirir; uzun menzilli kuvvetler (kütleçekim) boşlukta da etki eder. Dünyanın size uyguladığı kütleçekim kuvveti ağırlığınızdır. Aynı noktaya etki eden kuvvetlerin etkisi, vektörel toplamlarına, yani net (bileşke) kuvvete eşittir: Rx = ΣFx, Ry = ΣFy. Açıyı bulurken bileşenlerin işaretlerine bakarak doğru çeyreği seçin.

Superposition of forces. Several forces acting at one point on an object have the same effect as a single force equal to their vector sum, the net force:

R = ΣF = F₁ + F₂ + F₃ + … (4.1)
Rx = ΣFx, Ry = ΣFy (4.2)
R = √(Rx² + Ry²), tan θ = Ry/Rx

Add forces by components. Each component can be positive, negative or zero, and θ can land in any quadrant, so watch the signs.

Section 4.2

Newton’s first law

What happens when the net external force on an object is zero, and why inertial frames of reference matter.

An object cannot change its own motion by pushing on itself. Only external forces, exerted by other objects, affect it. So start with the simplest case: zero net external force.

Slide a puck across a table and it stops. The “common sense” conclusion is that moving things naturally come to rest and a force is needed to keep them going. Run the experiment on slicker surfaces and see what friction is really doing.

Bench · Same push, four surfaces

Figure 4.8

Each puck leaves your hand at 3.0 m/s. Only the friction force differs (illustrative values).

If friction could be eliminated entirely, the puck would never slow down. The idea that a force is needed to sustain motion is wrong.

NEWTON’S FIRST LAW. An object acted on by no net external force has a constant velocity (which may be zero) and zero acceleration.
ΣF = 0 (object in equilibrium) (4.3)

The tendency of an object to keep moving, or to stay at rest, is inertia: ketchup leaving a bottle you jerk back, a table setting that stays put when the cloth is yanked out. What matters is the net force. A book resting on a table has two forces on it, the table’s upward normal force and gravity, and they sum to zero. Zero net force behaves exactly like no force at all.

An object at rest or moving with constant velocity is in equilibrium.

TÜRKÇE

Newton’un birinci yasası: Üzerine net dış kuvvet etki etmeyen bir cismin hızı sabittir (sıfır olabilir) ve ivmesi sıfırdır: ΣF = 0. Hareketi sürdürmek için kuvvet gerekmez; masada kayan disk sürtünme yüzünden durur. Cismin hareket durumunu koruma eğilimine eylemsizlik denir. Önemli olan net kuvvettir: sıfır net kuvvet, hiç kuvvet olmamasıyla aynı sonucu verir. Durgun ya da sabit hızla hareket eden cisim dengededir.

Inertial frames of reference

Stand on roller skates in a bus that speeds up and you roll backward relative to the bus, with no net external force on you. Is the first law broken? No. The bus is accelerating relative to the earth, so it is not a valid frame for the first law. A frame in which the first law holds is an inertial frame. The earth’s surface is approximately one. Any frame moving at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is also inertial.

TÜRKÇE

Birinci yasanın geçerli olduğu çerçeveye eylemsiz referans çerçevesi denir. Yer yüzeyi yaklaşık olarak eylemsizdir; ona göre sabit hızla hareket eden her çerçeve de eylemsizdir. Hızlanan, yavaşlayan ya da dönen bir araç eylemsiz değildir: içindeki patenli yolcu itiliyormuş gibi görünür, ama onu iten bir cisim yoktur. Bu derste yalnızca eylemsiz çerçeveler kullanılır.

Section 4.3

Newton’s second law

How an object’s acceleration is set by the net external force and its mass.

Push a puck on frictionless ice with a constant net force. The puck accelerates in the direction of the net force, with constant acceleration. Reverse the force and the puck slows down: the acceleration still points along ΣF. This holds on curved paths too: a puck on a rope moving in a circle has ΣF and a both pointing to the centre.

Double the net force, double the acceleration. For a given object the ratio |ΣF|/a is constant. That ratio is the mass, a quantitative measure of inertia.

Bench · Puck on frictionless ice

Figures 4.12 & 4.14

Record points at different forces with the same mass: they fall on a straight line through the origin. Change the mass and the slope (1/m) changes. Try a negative force with an initial velocity to see Fig. 4.12c.

NEWTON’S SECOND LAW. If a net external force acts on an object, the object accelerates. The direction of acceleration is the same as the direction of the net external force. The mass times the acceleration vector equals the net external force vector.
ΣF = m a (4.6) ΣFx = m ax, ΣFy = m ay, ΣFz = m az (4.7)
1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

Four things to remember when you use it:

1. It is a vector equation; use it one component at a time. 2. Only external forces count. 3. It needs constant mass (variable mass waits for momentum, Ch. 8). 4. It holds only in inertial frames.

For the same net force, m₁a₁ = m₂a₂, so m₂/m₁ = a₁/a₂ (4.5). Masses fastened together add: m₁ + m₂.

TÜRKÇE

Newton’un ikinci yasası: Bir cisme net dış kuvvet etki ederse cisim, net kuvvetle aynı yönde ivmelenir: ΣF = m a, bileşenlerle ΣFx = m ax, ΣFy = m ay. Kütle, eylemsizliğin nicel ölçüsüdür; 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². Aynı kuvvet altında m₁a₁ = m₂a₂. Yasa vektöreldir, yalnızca dış kuvvetleri içerir, sabit kütle ve eylemsiz çerçeve gerektirir. Dikkat: ma bir kuvvet değildir; net kuvvetin sonucudur. Çözüm sırası: cismi seç, tüm dış kuvvetleri bul, eksen seç, bileşenleri topla, m·a’ya eşitle.

Common-sense trap: ma is not a force. Acceleration is the result of the net force. There is no “force of acceleration” pushing you into your seat. Your inertia keeps you where you were while the car accelerates around you.

Example 4.4 · Acceleration from force

A worker applies a constant horizontal force of 20 N to a 40 kg box resting on a level, freshly waxed floor with negligible friction. What is the acceleration of the box?

Identify & set up

Forces and acceleration → second law. Two kinds of force act: the weight w (earth) and contact forces from what touches the box: the worker’s push F and the floor’s normal force n. No friction. Take +x along the push, +y up. The box doesn’t move vertically, so ay = 0.

Execute

ΣFx = F + 0 + 0 = 20 N = m ax
ΣFy = 0 + n − w = m ay = 0
ax = 20 N / 40 kg = 0.50 m/s²

Evaluate

The net force is constant, so ax is constant and the Chapter 2 constant-acceleration equations apply. The y-equation shows n = w here.

Key concept: identify all external forces, choose axes, sum them, set the sum equal to m·a.

Example 4.5 · Force from acceleration

A waitress shoves a 0.45 kg ketchup bottle to her right along a level counter. It leaves her hand at 2.0 m/s and slides 1.0 m before stopping, slowed by a constant friction force. Find the magnitude and direction of the friction force.

Identify & set up

Forces: w down, n up, friction f opposite the motion. +x is the sliding direction. ax isn’t given, but it is constant, so use vx² = v0x² + 2ax(x − x₀) from Ch. 2.

Execute

ax = (0² − 2.0²) / (2 × 1.0 m) = −2.0 m/s²
ΣFx = −f = m ax = (0.45 kg)(−2.0 m/s²) = −0.90 N
f = 0.90 N, pointing left (opposite the velocity)

Evaluate

Flip the +x axis and you get ΣFx = +f, and again f = 0.90 N. Magnitudes don’t depend on your axes.

Careful: n = w in these two examples, but never assume it. In Chapter 5 they often differ.

Practice · Finding an unknown force (vector form)

Extra · builds on Eq. 4.7

Vectors can be written with unit vectors: F = Fx î + Fy ĵ, where î and ĵ point along +x and +y. ΣF = m a then holds for each component separately.

A 3.00 kg puck slides on a horizontal frictionless surface (top view) with acceleration a = (1.20 î − 0.800 ĵ) m/s². Exactly two horizontal forces act on it: F₁ = (−5.00 î + 6.00 ĵ) N and an unknown F₂. Find F₂.

Identify & set up

The object is the puck. Vertically, n and w cancel. Horizontally, the acceleration is given, so the second law tells you the net force. The unknown is one of the two forces that make up that net force, so subtract the known one, keeping signs.

Execute

ΣF = m a = (3.00 kg)(1.20 î − 0.800 ĵ) m/s² = (3.60 î − 2.40 ĵ) N
F₂ = ΣF − F₁ = (3.60 − (−5.00)) î + (−2.40 − 6.00) ĵ = (8.60 î − 8.40 ĵ) N
|F₂| = √(8.60² + 8.40²) = 12.0 N
arctan(−8.40/8.60) = −44.3°; F₂ₓ > 0, F₂ᵧ < 0 → quadrant IV → θ = 315.7° (44.3° below +x)

Evaluate

Check: F₁ + F₂ = (3.60 î − 2.40 ĵ) N, and dividing by 3.00 kg returns the given acceleration. Follow-up: if F₂ is replaced so the puck moves at constant velocity, the first law needs ΣF = 0, so the new force is −F₁ = (5.00 î − 6.00 ĵ) N. It is not −ΣF: that would only apply if a third force were added while F₂ stayed.

Section 4.4

Mass and weight

The difference between the mass of an object and its weight.

Mass characterises inertia: how hard it is to accelerate the object. Weight is a force: the gravitational pull of the earth (or whatever planet you are on). A freely falling 1 kg object accelerates at 9.81 m/s², so by the second law the force on it must be 9.81 N. In general:

w = m g (4.8) w = m g (vector form, 4.9)

g is the magnitude of the free-fall acceleration, so g and w are always positive. The weight acts all the time, whether the object is falling, hanging from a rope or resting on a table.

TÜRKÇE

Kütle cismin eylemsizliğidir ve yerden yere değişmez; ağırlık ise bir kuvvettir: w = mg. Ay’da (g = 1,62 m/s²) 1 kg’lık bir kütlenin ağırlığı 1,62 N’dur, kütlesi yine 1 kg’dır. Yaylı kantar ağırlığı ölçer ve yere göre farklı okur; eşit kollu terazi ağırlıkları karşılaştırdığı için her yerde aynı kütleyi gösterir. Ağırlık her zaman etkir: serbest düşen, ipte asılı ya da masada duran cisim için. “Bu kutu 6 kg çekiyor” demek yanlıştır: kutunun kütlesi 6 kg, ağırlığı yaklaşık 59 N’dur. Problemlerde m yerine asla ağırlığı koymayın.

Figures 4.22 & 4.23

The spring scale measures weight, so its reading changes with location. The equal-arm balance compares the object’s weight with known weights at the same place, so it reads the same mass everywhere. Inertial and gravitational mass are the same to better than 1 part in 10¹².

Bench · A coin tossed upward

Conceptual Example 4.6

The hand’s push is a contact force that vanishes the instant the coin leaves it. After that the only force is the weight. The velocity changes all the time; the net force ΣF = w never does.

Don’t confuse mass and weight. “This box weighs 6 kg” is sloppy. It means the box’s mass is 6 kg. Weight is in newtons, mass in kilograms.

Example 4.7 · Mass and weight

A truck with weight 2.49 × 10⁴ N travelling in the +x-direction makes an emergency stop. The x-component of the net external force on it is −1.83 × 10⁴ N. What is its acceleration? corrected from notes

Identify & set up

Use ΣFx = m ax. The newton is a force unit, so 2.49 × 10⁴ N is the weight, not the mass. Find m from w = mg first. The truck slows while moving in +x, so expect ax < 0.

Execute

m = w/g = 2.49 × 10⁴ N / 9.81 m/s² ≈ 2540 kg
ax = ΣFx g/w = (−1.83 × 10⁴ N)(9.81 m/s²) / (2.49 × 10⁴ N) ≈ −7.21 m/s²

Evaluate

Negative, as expected. That is a large deceleration; the seat belts work hard. Key concept: for m, use the mass, never the weight.

Practice · A rope pulling against weight

Extra · Eqs. 4.7, 4.8, 4.10

A 15.0 kg box hangs from a rope on Earth (g = 9.81 m/s²). At one instant the rope pulls up on it with a tension of 180 N. Gravity and the rope are the only forces. Take upward as positive. Find the box’s acceleration.

Identify & set up

Free-body diagram of the box: tension T = 180 N up (exerted by the rope) and weight w = mg down (exerted by the earth). Nothing else touches it. Use ΣFy = m ay.

Execute

w = mg = (15.0 kg)(9.81 m/s²) = 147.15 N
ΣFy = T − w = 180 N − 147.15 N = +32.85 N
ay = ΣFy/m = 32.85 N / 15.0 kg = +2.19 m/s² (upward)

Evaluate

The tension exceeds the weight, so the acceleration is upward. If T were 120 N, ay = (120 − 147.15)/15.0 = −1.81 m/s²: downward, although the rope still pulls up. If T = mg, ay = 0 (equilibrium). By the third law, the box pulls down on the rope with 180 N. That force acts on the rope, so it is not on the box’s diagram. On another planet, only w changes: use that planet’s g, and the mass stays the same.

Section 4.5

Newton’s third law

How the forces that two objects exert on each other are related.

NEWTON’S THIRD LAW. If object A exerts a force on object B (an “action”), then object B exerts a force on object A (a “reaction”). These two forces have the same magnitude but opposite directions. They act on different objects.
FA on B = −FB on A (4.10)

“Action” and “reaction” imply no cause and effect; either one can be called the action. The law holds for contact forces and for long-range forces: a falling table-tennis ball pulls up on the earth exactly as hard as the earth pulls down on it. The earth’s acceleration is microscopic only because its mass is enormous.

TÜRKÇE

Newton’un üçüncü yasası: A cismi B’ye bir kuvvet uygularsa, B de A’ya büyüklüğü aynı, yönü zıt bir kuvvet uygular: FA, B’ye = −FB, A’ya. Bu etki–tepki çifti her zaman farklı cisimlere etki eder; bu yüzden aynı serbest cisim diyagramında birbirini götürmez. Masadaki elmaya etki eden normal kuvvet, ağırlığın tepkisi değildir: ağırlığın tepkisi elmanın Dünya’yı yukarı çekmesidir. Üçüncü yasa tek başına hareket hakkında bilgi vermez; cismin hareketini yalnızca ona etki eden kuvvetler belirler. Birbirine dokunan iki arabada önce sistemin ivmesini, sonra tek bir araba için temas kuvvetini bulun.

Bench · The stonemason paradox

Conceptual Examples 4.10 & 4.11 · Figure 4.27

A mason pulls a 100 kg marble block with a light rope. The floor can hold the block with up to 200 N of friction; the mason’s non-skid soles hold him with up to 600 N (illustrative values). Horizontal forces only.

The moral. The third law relates forces on two different objects and by itself tells you nothing about motion. An object’s motion depends only on the forces acting on it. If the rope is in equilibrium or massless, the tension is the same everywhere: with 50 N at each end, the tension is 50 N, not 100 N.

Section 4.6

Free-body diagrams

How to use a free-body diagram to analyse the forces on an object.

1. The laws apply to a specific object. Decide first which object you are analysing.

2. Only forces acting on that object matter. For a walker, include the ground’s push on the person, not the person’s push on the ground.

3. Draw a free-body diagram. Show the object alone, “free” of its surroundings, with every force acting on it. Never put both forces of an action–reaction pair on the same diagram, and never include forces an object exerts on itself.

Test every arrow: what other object is applying this force? If you can’t name one, the force probably doesn’t exist (“force of acceleration”, “the ma force”).
TÜRKÇE

Serbest cisim diyagramı: (1) Hangi cismi incelediğinize baştan karar verin. (2) Yalnızca o cisme etki eden kuvvetleri alın; cismin başkalarına uyguladığı kuvvetler diyagrama girmez. (3) Cismi çevresinden ayırıp üzerine etki eden tüm kuvvetleri ok olarak çizin. Her ok için sorun: bu kuvveti hangi cisim uyguluyor? Cevap yoksa (“ivme kuvveti”, “ma kuvveti”) o kuvvet yoktur. Bir etki–tepki çiftinin iki kuvveti asla aynı diyagramda yer almaz.

Force as a vector

A quantitative measure of an interaction between two objects. Several forces act like their vector sum.

R = ΣF = F₁ + F₂ + … (4.1)

Zero net external force ⇒ equilibrium, zero acceleration. Inertial frames only.

The gravitational force of the earth on an object. Weight depends on location; mass does not.
