1The two products, side by sideİki çarpım yan yana

Vectors are not numbers, so ordinary multiplication does not apply to them. Chapter 1 defines two products, and they answer two different physical questions. Both start the same way: draw $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ tail to tail and call the angle between them $\phi$, from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.

Scalar (dot) product $\vec A\cdot\vec B$Vector (cross) product $\vec A\times\vec B$
Resulta scalar — a signed numbera vector, perpendicular to the plane of $\vec A$ and $\vec B$
From magnitudes and the angle$AB\cos\phi$  (1.16)magnitude $AB\sin\phi$  (1.20); direction by the right-hand rule
From components$A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z$  (1.19)$(A_yB_z-A_zB_y,\ A_zB_x-A_xB_z,\ A_xB_y-A_yB_x)$  (1.25)
Geometric reading$A$ times the part of $\vec B$ along $\vec A$: $A(B\cos\phi)$$A$ times the part of $\vec B$ perpendicular to $\vec A$: the area of the parallelogram they span
Zero whenthe vectors are perpendicular $(\phi=90^\circ)$the vectors are parallel or antiparallel $(\phi=0^\circ$ or $180^\circ)$
Largest whenparallel: $+AB$ (antiparallel gives $-AB$)perpendicular: $AB$
Ordercommutative: $\vec A\cdot\vec B=\vec B\cdot\vec A$anticommutative: $\vec A\times\vec B=-\vec B\times\vec A$
Physics aheadwork, $W=\vec F\cdot\vec s$ (Week 05)torque, $\vec\tau=\vec r\times\vec F$ (Week 11); magnetic force (Physics II)
Key equations (1.16) and (1.19)
$$\vec A\cdot\vec B=AB\cos\phi=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z$$
Key equations (1.20) and (1.25)
$$\begin{gathered}|\vec A\times\vec B|=AB\sin\phi,\\ C_x=A_yB_z-A_zB_y,\quad C_y=A_zB_x-A_xB_z,\quad C_z=A_xB_y-A_yB_x\end{gathered}$$
Watch out

Caution — $AB\cos\phi$ against $AB\sin\phi$. Hold the magnitudes fixed and turn one vector: where one product is largest the other is zero. The textbook says so in one sentence; the last animation on this page lets you watch it happen.

2The scalar productSkaler çarpım

Review example 1 · Y&F Example 1.9

Calculating a scalar product two ways

Problem. Two vectors lie in the $xy$-plane: $A=4.00$ at $53.0^\circ$ and $B=5.00$ at $130.0^\circ$, both angles measured from $+x$ toward $+y$. Find $\vec A\cdot\vec B$.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Identify and set up. Two routes are available — equation (1.16) from the magnitudes and the angle between them, equation (1.19) from the components — and doing both checks each against the other.

Limiting check. The angle between the vectors is under $90^\circ$, so the answer must be positive and smaller than $AB=20.0$.

Execute. The angle between them is $\phi=130.0^\circ-53.0^\circ=77.0^\circ$, so

$$\vec A\cdot\vec B=AB\cos\phi=(4.00)(5.00)\cos77.0^\circ=4.50.$$

For the component route, both angles are already measured from $+x$ toward $+y$, so equations (1.5) apply directly. Keeping an extra figure in the components and rounding at the end:

$$\begin{aligned}A_x&=4.00\cos53.0^\circ=2.407, & A_y&=4.00\sin53.0^\circ=3.195,\\ B_x&=5.00\cos130.0^\circ=-3.214, & B_y&=5.00\sin130.0^\circ=3.830,\end{aligned}$$ $$\begin{aligned}\vec A\cdot\vec B&=A_xB_x+A_yB_y\\&=(2.407)(-3.214)+(3.195)(3.830)=4.50.\end{aligned}$$

Evaluate. The two routes agree. Notice that the $x$-term was negative (the $x$-components point opposite ways) and the $y$-term positive and larger; the sum came out positive, exactly as $\phi\lt 90^\circ$ demanded.

TR: İki yol da 4.50 verir: açıdan $(\phi=77.0^\circ)$ ya da bileşenlerden. Ara bileşenlerde fazladan bir basamak taşı; $x$ terimi negatif, $y$ terimi pozitif ve daha büyük.

Predict, then watch

The projection that the dot product multiplies

Example 1.9 with both vectors live. The green segment is $B\cos\phi$, the part of $\vec B$ along $\vec A$; the readouts compute $AB\cos\phi$ and $A_xB_x+A_yB_y$ side by side. The other cases are VP1.10.1, VP1.10.2 and the two cables of problem P8.

Drag $\theta_B$ past $\theta_A+90^\circ$ and watch the green segment flip to the far side of the origin as the product changes sign. Equal magnitudes and a $180^\circ$ separation give $-AB$; a $90^\circ$ separation gives zero whatever the magnitudes.

Aradaki açı 90°'yi geçince yeşil izdüşüm ters tarafa geçer ve çarpım işaret değiştirir.

Review example 2 · Y&F Example 1.10

Finding an angle with the scalar product

Problem. Find the angle between $\vec A=2.00\hat\imath+3.00\hat\jmath+1.00\hat k$ and $\vec B=-4.00\hat\imath+2.00\hat\jmath-1.00\hat k$.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Identify and set up. Solve equation (1.16) for $\cos\phi$; the product comes from (1.19) and the magnitudes from (1.11):

$$\cos\phi=\frac{\vec A\cdot\vec B}{AB}=\frac{A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z}{AB}.$$

Limiting check. Whatever the numbers, $\cos\phi$ must land between $-1$ and $+1$; if it does not, the arithmetic is wrong. A negative value means an obtuse angle.

Execute.

$$\begin{gathered}\vec A\cdot\vec B=(2.00)(-4.00)+(3.00)(2.00)+(1.00)(-1.00)=-3.00,\\ A=\sqrt{14.00},\qquad B=\sqrt{21.00},\end{gathered}$$ $$\cos\phi=\frac{-3.00}{\sqrt{14.00}\sqrt{21.00}}=-0.175,\qquad\phi=100^\circ.$$

Evaluate. The scalar product was negative, so $\phi$ had to lie between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ — a free check on the answer before the inverse cosine was taken. The formula works for any two vectors whose components are known, in two dimensions or three.

TR: $\cos\phi=(\vec A\cdot\vec B)/(AB)$. Çarpım negatif çıktıysa açı geniştir: $\phi=100^\circ$.

Predict, then watch

The angle between two vectors in three dimensions

The vectors of Example 1.10 drawn from a common origin. Turn and tilt the view: the angle between them does not change, only its appearance does — which is why it is computed from the components rather than read off a drawing. The other cases are VP1.10.3 (almost perpendicular), the P9 pair (exactly perpendicular) and the two vectors of Example 1.8.

Tilt the view until you look straight down the plane of the two vectors: the arc collapses to a line and the angle cannot be read at all. The components never had that problem.

Görünüm dönerken açı değişmez; bileşenlerden hesaplanan açı görünümden bağımsızdır.

Review example 3 · VP1.10.2

The angle from the components, in the plane

Problem. Vector $\vec C$ has magnitude 6.50 at $55.0^\circ$ counter-clockwise from $+x$. Vector $\vec D$ has components $D_x=+4.80$, $D_y=-8.40$. (a) Find $\vec C\cdot\vec D$. (b) Find the angle between them.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Predict. $\vec C$ is in the first quadrant and $\vec D$ in the fourth, so the angle between them is large and the product negative.

Execute. Components of $\vec C$ from equations (1.5), then (1.19), then (1.16) rearranged:

$$\begin{gathered}C_x=6.50\cos55.0^\circ=3.728,\qquad C_y=6.50\sin55.0^\circ=5.325,\\ \vec C\cdot\vec D=(3.728)(4.80)+(5.325)(-8.40)=-26.8,\end{gathered}$$ $$\begin{gathered}D=\sqrt{4.80^2+8.40^2}=9.675,\\ \cos\phi=\frac{-26.8}{(6.50)(9.675)}=-0.427,\qquad \phi=115^\circ.\end{gathered}$$

Evaluate. Obtuse, as the quadrants promised. The product is numerically the same as in VP1.10.1 $(-26.8)$ although the vectors are entirely different: one scalar cannot tell you the two vectors it came from. Select the VP1.10.2 case in the first animation to see this pair.

TR: $\vec C$ birinci, $\vec D$ dördüncü bölgede: açı geniş $(115^\circ)$, çarpım negatif $(-26.8)$.

Review example 4 · VP1.10.4

A scalar product read backwards: work with one unknown component

Problem. The work done by a force $\vec F$ on an object that moves through displacement $\vec s$ is $W=\vec F\cdot\vec s$. An object moves through $\vec s=(4.00\ \mathrm m)\hat\imath+(5.00\ \mathrm m)\hat\jmath$ under a force whose $x$-component is $F_x=-12.0\ \mathrm N$. The work done is $26.0\ \mathrm J$. (a) Find $F_y$. (b) Find the angle between $\vec F$ and $\vec s$.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Predict. The $x$-part of the work is $(-12.0)(4.00)=-48.0\ \mathrm J$, yet the total is $+26.0\ \mathrm J$, so the $y$-part must be about $+74\ \mathrm J$ and $F_y$ positive, around 15 N.

Symbolic route. Equation (1.19) is one equation in one unknown:

$$W=F_xs_x+F_ys_y\ \Longrightarrow\ F_y=\frac{W-F_xs_x}{s_y},\qquad \cos\phi=\frac{W}{Fs}.$$

Limiting check. If $W$ equalled $F_xs_x$ exactly, the numerator would vanish and $F_y=0$: a purely horizontal force. Any work beyond that must come from $F_y$.

Execute.

$$\begin{gathered}F_y=\frac{26.0-(-12.0)(4.00)}{5.00}\ \mathrm N=14.8\ \mathrm N,\\ F=\sqrt{12.0^2+14.8^2}=19.1\ \mathrm N,\qquad s=\sqrt{4.00^2+5.00^2}=6.40\ \mathrm m,\end{gathered}$$ $$\cos\phi=\frac{26.0}{(19.1)(6.40)}=0.213,\qquad\phi=77.7^\circ.$$

Evaluate. The force is nearly, but not quite, perpendicular to the displacement, which is why a 19.1 N force over 6.40 m does only 26.0 J instead of the 122 J it would do if aligned. Only $F\cos\phi=4.06\ \mathrm N$ counts, and $(4.06\ \mathrm N)(6.40\ \mathrm m)=26.0\ \mathrm J$. This is the whole idea of Week 05, reached with nothing but §1.10.

TR: Bilinmeyen bileşen için skaler çarpım tek bilinmeyenli bir denklemdir: $F_y=14.8$ N. Kuvvet yer değiştirmeye neredeyse dik $(77.7^\circ)$, bu yüzden iş küçüktür.

Predict, then watch

Work is the part of the force along the displacement

The displacement of VP1.10.4 is fixed and $F_x=-12.0$ N is fixed; slide $F_y$. The thick segment is $F\cos\phi$, the component of $\vec F$ along $\vec s$ — green when it helps the motion, red when it opposes it — and $W$ is that component times $s$.

Somewhere between $F_y=0$ and $F_y=14.8$ N the work passes through zero: at that instant the force is perpendicular to the displacement and does nothing, however large it is.

$F_y$ değişirken iş sıfırdan geçer: o anda kuvvet yer değiştirmeye diktir ve büyüklüğü ne olursa olsun iş yapmaz.

Check yourself · scalar product (Y&F Test your understanding 1.10)

$\vec A$ has magnitude 2 and $\vec B$ magnitude 3, so $AB=6$. For each case, which angle $\phi$ — (i) $0^\circ$, (ii) $90^\circ$ or (iii) $180^\circ$ — is required? (a) $\vec A\cdot\vec B=0$; (c) $\vec A\cdot\vec B=6$; (d) $\vec A\cdot\vec B=-6$.

Answers

(a) (ii) $90^\circ$: the scalar product is zero only for perpendicular vectors. (c) (i) $0^\circ$: it equals $+AB$ only for parallel vectors. (d) (iii) $180^\circ$: it equals $-AB$ only for antiparallel vectors.

Review example 5 · Y&F Example 1.11

Calculating a vector product two ways

Problem. $\vec A$ has magnitude 6 units along $+x$. $\vec B$ has magnitude 4 units in the $xy$-plane at $30^\circ$ from $+x$. Find $\vec C=\vec A\times\vec B$.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Identify and set up. Magnitude from equation (1.20) and direction from the right-hand rule; then the components from equations (1.25) as a check.

Limiting check. At $\phi=0^\circ$ the product would vanish; at $\phi=90^\circ$ it would be largest, $AB=24$. At $30^\circ$ expect something well below 24, pointing out of the page because the rotation from $\vec A$ to $\vec B$ is counter-clockwise.

Execute.

$$\begin{gathered}C=AB\sin\phi=(6)(4)\sin30^\circ=12,\\ \text{direction: }+z,\qquad\vec C=12\hat k.\end{gathered}$$

By components, $A_x=6$, $A_y=A_z=0$; $B_x=4\cos30^\circ=2\sqrt3$, $B_y=4\sin30^\circ=2$, $B_z=0$:

$$\begin{aligned}C_x&=A_yB_z-A_zB_y=0,\\ C_y&=A_zB_x-A_xB_z=0,\\ C_z&=A_xB_y-A_yB_x=(6)(2)-(0)(2\sqrt3)=12.\end{aligned}$$

Evaluate. Both routes give $12\hat k$. Which is more convenient depends on what is given: magnitudes and an angle favour (1.20); components favour (1.25).

TR: $C=AB\sin30^\circ=12$, sağ el kuralı $+z$; bileşenlerle $C_z=A_xB_y-A_yB_x=12$. Düzlemdeki iki vektörün çarpımı sayfadan dışarı ya da içeri bakar.

Predict, then watch

The parallelogram, and which way its normal points

Example 1.11 live: $\vec A$ fixed along $+x$, $\vec B$ on a slider. The shaded parallelogram has area $AB\sin\phi$; the marker at its centre is $\odot$ for out of the page and $\otimes$ for into it. Switch the order to see $\vec B\times\vec A$.

Take $\vec B$ below the $x$-axis and the marker flips to $\otimes$ with $C_z$ negative; take it to $0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$ and the parallelogram collapses to a line with no area and no product.

B ekseni geçince işaret döner; 0° ve 180°'de paralelkenar bir çizgiye iner ve çarpım sıfırdır.

Review example 6 · problem P9

Torque: where a vector product turns something

Problem. A mechanic grips a wrench at $\vec r=0.320\ \mathrm m$ at $35.0^\circ$ above $+x$ from the bolt, and pulls with $95.0\ \mathrm N$ in the $-y$ direction. (a) Find $\vec\tau=\vec r\times\vec F$. (b) Its magnitude, and into or out of the page? (c) Which pull direction, at the same magnitude, gives the largest $|\vec\tau|$? (d) Is there a pull that produces no torque?

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

Execute. $\vec r=(0.320\cos35.0^\circ,\ 0.320\sin35.0^\circ)=(0.262,\ 0.184)\ \mathrm m$ and $\vec F=(0,\ -95.0)\ \mathrm N$. With $r_z=F_x=F_z=0$ only $\tau_z$ survives in equations (1.25):

$$\begin{gathered}\tau_z=r_xF_y-r_yF_x=(0.262)(-95.0)-0=-24.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m},\\ \vec\tau=(-24.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m})\hat k.\end{gathered}$$

(b) $|\vec\tau|=24.9\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$, into the page: a clockwise turning effect, which is what pulling down on a lever tilted up and to the right should give. (c) $\tau=rF\sin\phi$ is largest at $\phi=90^\circ$, pulling perpendicular to the wrench: at $125.0^\circ$ or $305.0^\circ$ from $+x$, both giving $rF=(0.320)(95.0)=30.4\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$; only $305.0^\circ$ keeps the clockwise sense. The actual pull achieves $24.9/30.4=82\%$ because $\phi=125^\circ$ and $\sin125^\circ=0.819$. (d) Yes: along the wrench, $\phi=0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, the line of the force passes through the bolt and $\sin\phi=0$.

TR: Düzlemde yalnızca $\tau_z=r_xF_y-r_yF_x=-24.9$ N·m kalır: sayfaya doğru, saat yönünde. En büyük tork kola dik çekişte $(rF=30.4$ N·m), kol boyunca çekişte sıfır.

Predict, then watch

Only the perpendicular part of the force turns the wrench

Problem P9 live: the wrench is fixed at $35^\circ$; turn the force direction. The purple segment is $F\sin\phi$, the part of $\vec F$ perpendicular to $\vec r$; the curved arrow at the bolt shows the sense of $\vec\tau$.

The readout is $\tau_z=r_xF_y-r_yF_x$, which is $rF\sin\phi$ with the sign built in. It peaks at $125^\circ$ and $305^\circ$, passes through zero at $35^\circ$ and $215^\circ$, and the sense flips each time it does.

Tork kola dik çekişte en büyük, kol boyunca çekişte sıfırdır; sıfırdan geçerken dönüş yönü değişir.

Review example 7 · three dimensions

A vector product by components, and its two checks

Problem. $\vec A=2.00\hat\imath-1.00\hat\jmath+3.00\hat k$ and $\vec B=-1.00\hat\imath+4.00\hat\jmath+2.00\hat k$. (a) Show that they are perpendicular. (b) Find $\vec A\times\vec B$. (c) Check its magnitude against $AB\sin\phi$ and its direction against both factors. (d) Write a unit vector perpendicular to both.

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

(a) $\vec A\cdot\vec B=(2)(-1)+(-1)(4)+(3)(2)=-2-4+6=0$: perpendicular, so $\phi=90^\circ$ and $\sin\phi=1$.

(b) Equations (1.25), cyclic $x\to y\to z\to x$, minus sign distributed over both terms of the middle bracket:

$$\begin{aligned}C_x&=A_yB_z-A_zB_y=(-1)(2)-(3)(4)=-14,\\ C_y&=A_zB_x-A_xB_z=(3)(-1)-(2)(2)=-7,\\ C_z&=A_xB_y-A_yB_x=(2)(4)-(-1)(-1)=7,\end{aligned}$$ $$\vec A\times\vec B=-14\hat\imath-7\hat\jmath+7\hat k.$$

(c) $|\vec C|=\sqrt{196+49+49}=\sqrt{294}=17.1$, and $AB\sin90^\circ=\sqrt{14}\sqrt{21}=\sqrt{294}$: they agree. Then $\vec C\cdot\vec A=(-14)(2)+(-7)(-1)+(7)(3)=-28+7+21=0$ and $\vec C\cdot\vec B=14-28+14=0$: perpendicular to both, as a vector product must be. Those two dot products are the fastest check on any cross product you compute.

(d) $\hat n=\vec C/C=(-0.816,\ -0.408,\ 0.408)$; $-\hat n$ is the other one.

TR: Bileşen formülü döngüseldir $(x\to y\to z\to x)$. Sonucu iki şekilde kontrol et: büyüklük $AB\sin\phi$'ye eşit mi, ve $\vec C\cdot\vec A=\vec C\cdot\vec B=0$ mı.

Check yourself · vector product (Y&F Test your understanding 1.10)

Again $A=2$, $B=3$. Which $\phi$ — (i) $0^\circ$, (ii) $90^\circ$, (iii) $180^\circ$ — is required for (b) $\vec A\times\vec B=\vec 0$, and for (e) $|\vec A\times\vec B|=6$? Then: can two nonzero vectors have both $\vec A\cdot\vec B=0$ and $\vec A\times\vec B=\vec 0$?

Answers

(b) (i) or (iii): the vector product vanishes only for parallel or antiparallel vectors. (e) (ii): its magnitude reaches $AB$ only for perpendicular vectors. Both zero: impossible for nonzero vectors — the first needs $\phi=90^\circ$, the second needs $\phi=0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, and no angle is both (Y&F Q1.18).

4One angle, two productsBir açı, iki çarpım

Review example 8

When are the two products equal?

Problem. Two vectors have fixed magnitudes $A$ and $B$ and the angle $\theta$ between them runs from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$. (a) At $\theta=30.0^\circ$, which is larger: the scalar product or the magnitude of the vector product? (b) For which $\theta$ are they equal? (c) For which $\theta$ is the scalar product twice the magnitude of the vector product?

Show the worked route / Çözüm yolunu göster

(a) $AB\cos30.0^\circ=0.866\,AB$ against $AB\sin30.0^\circ=0.500\,AB$: the scalar product. (b) $AB\cos\theta=AB\sin\theta$ needs $\tan\theta=1$: $\theta=45^\circ$; and $|AB\cos\theta|=AB\sin\theta$ also at $\theta=135^\circ$, where the scalar product is $-0.707\,AB$. (c) $AB\cos\theta=2AB\sin\theta$ gives $\tan\theta=\tfrac12$, $\theta=26.6^\circ$. None of these depends on $A$ or $B$: the magnitudes cancel, and only the angle decides how the two products share the total $AB$.

TR: $\tan\theta=1$ için $45^\circ$ (ve $135^\circ$); $\tan\theta=\tfrac12$ için $26.6^\circ$. Büyüklükler sadeleşir; paylaşımı yalnızca açı belirler.

Predict, then watch

One angle, two products

As the angle between two vectors grows, $AB\cos\phi$ falls from $+AB$ through zero to $-AB$ while $AB\sin\phi$ rises from zero to $AB$ and back. The two curves cross at $45^\circ$.

Each product vanishes exactly where the other is extreme. That is why one of them measures alignment — work, in Week 05 — and the other measures turning: torque, in Week 11.

Biri sıfırken öteki en büyüktür: biri hizalanmayı, öteki döndürmeyi ölçer.

Choosing the product

Ask what the physical question is. How much of one vector lies along another? — a projection, an alignment, an energy transfer: the scalar product. How much of one vector acts across another, and about which axis? — an area, a turning effect, a normal to a plane: the vector product. Then check the answer against the table above: the sign of a scalar product tells you whether the angle is acute or obtuse before you compute it, and a cross product must come out perpendicular to both factors.
